Welcome to Module 2! Now that you know the special letters of the Megrelian alphabet, especially the glottal stop (**ჸ** *ʔ*) and the reduced vowel[^2] (**ჷ** *ə*), it is time to start communicating.

Most people in Samegrelo also speak Georgian, and everyday Megrelian freely mixes in Georgian words. Where a Georgian form is common, this lesson shows it next to the Megrelian one and says which is which.

---

## 1. Hello and Goodbye

| Megrelian | Transliteration | English Meaning |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **გამარჯობა!** | gamarjoba | Hello (the Georgian greeting, used in Samegrelo too) |
| **გომორძგუა!** | gomordzgua | "Victory!": the Megrelian counterpart of გამარჯობა, also a toast |
| **ძირაფაშახ!** | dzirapashakh | Goodbye! (literally "until seeing") |

> **🗣️ Language Note:** **გომორძგუა** (*gomordzgua*) is the Megrelian word for "victory" (Georgian *gamarjveba*), and Georgian **გამარჯობა** is built on the same idea. You will also hear the fuller wish **გომორძგუა ჯგირი სქანი!** (*gomordzgua jgiri skani!*, "good victory to you!").

Two words to learn now, ready for when we meet the time-of-day greetings later: **ოჭუმარე** (*och'umare*, morning) and **სერი** (*seri*, night).

## 2. "How are you?"

The question is built from **მუჭო** (*much'o*), "how," and the verb "to be."

### Asking
*   **მუჭო რექ?** (much'o rek?) — How are you? *(to one person)*
*   **მუჭო რეთ?** (much'o ret?) — How are you? *(to several people[^1])*

### Replying
*   **ჯგირო!** (jgiro!) — Well!
*   **ძაამი ჯგირი!** (dzaami jgiri!) — Very good!
*   **გლახათ.** (glakhat) — Badly.

> **🗣️ Language Note:** **ჯგირო** (*jgiro*) is the adverb "well"; the adjective "good" is **ჯგირი** (*jgiri*), which some speakers say **ჯგჷრი** (*jgəri*).

---

## 3. Introductions: "What is your name?"

Like Georgian, Megrelian asks "What are you called?" The verb **ჯოხო** (*jokho*) means "is called."

*   **მუ რჯოხო?** (mu rjokho?) — What is your name?
*   **მა მიჯოხო [Name].** (ma mijokho...) — My name is [Name]. You will also hear **მა ბჯოხო [Name]** (*ma bjokho*).
*   **დო სი მუ რჯოხო?** (do si mu rjokho?) — And you, what is your name?

## 4. Basic Courtesies

| Megrelian | Transliteration | English |
|-----------|-----------------|---------|
| **მარდობა** | mardoba | Thank you |
| **მადლობა** | madloba | Thank you (the same word as Georgian; common in Megrelian too) |
| **დიდი მარდობელი ვორექ** | didi mardobeli vorek | Thank you very much (literally "I am very grateful") |
| **ქო** / **ჰო** | ko / ho | Yes |
| **ვარი** / **ვარ** | vari / var | No |
| **ქორთხულე** | kortkhule | Please (literally "I ask you") |
| **ბოდიში** | bodishi | Sorry / Excuse me |

## 5. Terms of Endearment

*   **ჩქიმი შური დო გური** (chkimi shuri do guri) — My soul and heart. *(Deep affection.)*
*   **გოლუაფირო** (goluapiro) and **სი ქიგგალე** (si kiggale) — Darling, my dear: Megrelian counterparts of Georgian **გენაცვალე** (*genatsvale*), which you will hear in Samegrelo too.

## 6. Practice Dialogue

Try reading this dialogue between **გიორგი** (Giorgi) and **თამუნა** (Tamuna). Look out for the glottal stop in **მაჸალე** (*maʔale*), "friend."

**გიორგი (Giorgi):** გომორძგუა, მაჸალე! (Gomordzgua, maʔale! — *Hello, friend!*)
**თამუნა (Tamuna):** გომორძგუა! მუჭო რექ? (Gomordzgua! Much'o rek? — *Hello! How are you?*)
**გიორგი (Giorgi):** ჯგირო, მარდობა. სი მუჭო რექ? (Jgiro, mardoba. Si much'o rek? — *Well, thanks. How are you?*)
**თამუნა (Tamuna):** ძაამი ჯგირი! (Dzaami jgiri! — *Very good!*)
**გიორგი (Giorgi):** მუ რჯოხო? (Mu rjokho? — *What is your name?*)
**თამუნა (Tamuna):** მა მიჯოხო თამუნა. (Ma mijokho Tamuna. — *My name is Tamuna.*)
**გიორგი (Giorgi):** ძირაფაშახ! (Dzirapashakh! — *Goodbye!*)

### Next Steps
- Memorize the Megrelian word for "how": **მუჭო** (much'o).
- Try introducing yourself: **მა მიჯოხო...** (ma mijokho...).

Once you are comfortable with these greetings, you have completed the current introductory modules for Megrelian!

[^1]: **Plural** (მრავლობითი რიცხვი, *mravlobiti ritskhvi*) — A grammatical form indicating more than one person or thing.
[^2]: **Vowel** (ხმოვანი, *khmovani*) — A speech sound made with your mouth open, not blocked by your teeth, tongue, or lips.
