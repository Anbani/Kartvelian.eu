Welcome to Megrelian (მარგალური ნინა, *Margaluri nina*)! 

Megrelian is a Kartvelian language spoken in Samegrelo, in western Georgia, and in neighbouring parts of Abkhazia. Its closest relative is Laz; together they form the Zan branch of the family. Georgian is a more distant sister: the two split long ago, and a Georgian speaker cannot understand Megrelian without study, much like an English speaker cannot automatically understand German.

The good news for learners: **Megrelian is written in the same Mkhedruli script as Georgian.** If you have already studied Georgian, you can read almost every Megrelian word you meet.

In this module, we briefly review the alphabet and then focus on the **three extra letters** that Megrelian adds to it.

---

## 1. The Foundation: The 33 Georgian Letters

Megrelian uses all 33 letters of the standard Georgian alphabet. The rules you learned for Georgian still apply:
*   **No uppercase or lowercase letters** in ordinary writing.
*   **One letter, one sound:** spelling follows pronunciation closely.
*   **The Ejectives remain:** You still need to "pop" your consonants for `პ, ტ, კ, წ, ჭ, ყ` (*p', t', k', ts', ch', q'*).

*(If you have not learned the Mkhedruli script yet, we highly recommend completing Module 1 of the Survival Georgian course before continuing!)*

---

## 2. The Three Extra Letters

Dictionaries of Megrelian, such as Otar Kajaia's *Megrelian–Georgian Dictionary*, write the language with the 33 Georgian letters plus **three extra letters**. None of the three is unique to Megrelian: Svan uses them too, and **ჲ** comes from Old Georgian.

| Letter | Name | Transliteration | IPA | English Approximation | 
|--------|------|-----------------|-----|-----------------------|
| `ჸ` | Elifi | ʔ | /ʔ/ | The "catch" in **uh-oh** |
| `ჲ` | Hie (yota) | y | /j/ | The 'y' in **y**es |
| `ჷ` | Yn (schwa) | ə | /ə/ | The 'a' in **a**bout |

Let's break them down one by one.

---

## 3. The Glottal Stop: `ჸ` (*ʔ*, Elifi)

You will meet this letter often, in some of the most common words of the language.

**How to pronounce it:** 
`ჸ` (*ʔ*) is a **glottal stop**, a consonant[^1] made by briefly closing your throat and stopping the airflow.
*   Say the English phrase **"Uh-oh"**. Notice the sharp, tiny pause between the two syllables? That closure of the throat is the `ჸ` (*ʔ*) sound.

`ჸ` often stands where Georgian has `ყ` (*q'*): compare Megrelian **ჸვალი** (*ʔvali*, cheese) with Georgian **ყველი** (*q'veli*), or **ჸურზენი** (*ʔurzeni*, grapes) with Georgian **ყურძენი** (*q'urdzeni*). Unlike a vowel[^3], it always closes the airflow. Dialects differ: the Senaki dialects use `ჸ` more than Zugdidi–Samurzaqano, which sometimes keeps `ყ`, and between vowels `ჸ` is often dropped altogether.

**Practice Words:**
*   **<mark>ჸ</mark>უდე** (ʔude) — House
*   **<mark>ჸ</mark>ვა** (ʔva) — Forehead
*   **<mark>ჸ</mark>ოროფა** (ʔoropa) — Love
*   **მა<mark>ჸ</mark>ალე** (maʔale) — Friend

> **🗣️ Pronunciation Tip:** Do not confuse `ჸ` (*ʔ*, glottal stop) with `ყ` (*q'*, the ejective[^2] 'q' made at the back of the throat). `ყ` (*q'*) has a distinct popping sound; `ჸ` (*ʔ*) is only a sudden blockage of air.

---

## 4. The Yota: `ჲ` (*y*, Hie)

Standard Georgian has no separate letter for the "y" sound. Megrelian writes it with `ჲ`, a letter of Old Georgian: the 'y' of English *yellow* or *yes*.

**Practice Word:**
*   **ქ<mark>ჲ</mark>ანა** (kyana) — Country, world *(a variant of **ქიანა**, kiana)*

---

## 5. The Reduced Vowel: `ჷ` (*ə*, Yn)

If you speak English, you already know this sound. It is the relaxed, neutral "uh" in the 'a' of *about*, or the 'u' in *supply*.

Standard Georgian has five vowels and no /ə/. Megrelian, especially in the Zugdidi–Samurzaqano dialects, has `ჷ` (*ə*) as well, both inside words and at their ends, where other dialects often have `ი` or `უ`.

**Practice Words:**
*   **ცჷრა** (tsəra) — Girl *(also **ცირა**, tsira)*
*   **ჯგჷრი** (jgəri) — Good *(also **ჯგირი**, jgiri)*

## 6. First Reading Practice: Basic Megrelian Words

Let's put your reading skills to the test. Here are a few everyday Megrelian words next to their Georgian equivalents.

| Megrelian | Transliteration | English Meaning | Georgian Equivalent |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| მა | ma | I | მე (me) |
| სი | si | You | შენ (shen) |
| მუჭო? | much'o? | How? | როგორ? (rogor?) |
| ჯგირო | jgiro | Well | კარგად (k'argad) |
| ბოში | boshi | Boy | ბიჭი (bich'i) |
| ძღაბი | dzghabi | Girl | გოგო (gogo) |
| კატუ | k'at'u | Cat | კატა (k'at'a) |
| ჯოღორი | joghori | Dog | ძაღლი (dzaghli) |

### Test Your Pronunciation: The Greeting
Here is how to ask "How are you?" with a word from the table above:

**მუჭო რექ? (Much'o rek?)** — How are you?
**ჯგირო! (Jgiro!)** — Well!

### Next Steps
- Practice saying "Uh-oh" to find your glottal stop.
- Read the word **ჸუდე** (ʔude, house) out loud three times, using the glottal stop and not a "k" or "q" sound. 
- Memorize the words for "I" (**მა**, *ma*) and "You" (**სი**, *si*).

Once you are comfortable reading the three new letters, you are ready to start speaking in **Module 2: Greetings in Megrelian!**

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Consonant** (Georgian: თანხმოვანი, *tankhmovani*) — A speech sound made by narrowing or closing the mouth or throat.
[^2]: **Ejective** (Georgian: მკვეთრი, *mk'vetri*) — A consonant pronounced with the throat closed, so the air comes out in a sharp pop rather than a puff.
[^3]: **Vowel** (Georgian: ხმოვანი, *khmovani*) — A speech sound made with your mouth open, not blocked by your teeth, tongue, or lips.
