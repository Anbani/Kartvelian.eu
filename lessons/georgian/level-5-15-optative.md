Welcome to Level 5 and Module 15! Up until now, when talking about your own actions, you have mostly been speaking in the indicative mood—talking about hard reality: things you *are doing*, things you *did*, and things you *will do*. 

But what about things you *want* to do? Or things you *must* do? 

To talk about desires, obligations, and hypothetical situations, Georgian uses a special verb[^16] form called the **Optative[^10] Mood** (კავშირებითი - *k'avshirebiti*). If you have studied French or Spanish, you can think of this as the Georgian Subjunctive. It is the mood of potential.

---

## 1. The Three Triggers

You rarely use the Optative mood on its own. It is almost always triggered by a specific modal word or phrase that comes right before it. The three most common triggers you will use every day are:

1.  **უნდა (unda) — Must / Should**
    *   *Example:* I **must** go. 
2.  **მინდა, რომ... (minda, rom...) — I want (that)...**
    *   *Example:* I want **to** sleep. *(Literally in Georgian: I want that I sleep).*
3.  **შემიძლია (shemidzlia) — I can / I am able to...**
    *   *Example:* I can **help** you. 

Whenever you use one of these triggers, the action verb that follows it **must** be pushed into the Optative mood.

---

## 2. Forming the Optative (Regular Verbs)

The easiest way to form the Optative is to build it from the **Aorist[^14]** stem (Module 13) or the **Future Tense[^4]** (Module 12). 

For regular verbs, take the Aorist form of the same person (Module 13) and swap its final vowel for the Optative ending: usually 1st/2nd person[^2] singular `-ე` (*-e*) → **-ო** (*-o*, **გავაკეთე** *gavak'ete* → **გავაკეთო** *gavak'eto*), 3rd person[^6] singular `-ა` (*-a*) → **-ოს** (*-os*, **გააკეთა** *gaak'eta* → **გააკეთოს** *gaak'etos*), `-ეთ` (*-et*) → **-ოთ** (*-ot*), and `-ეს` (*-es*) → **-ონ** (*-on*). Verbs with other present/future suffixes (`-ავ` *-av*, `-ამ` *-am*, `-ობ` *-ob*) drop them the same way the Aorist does: **დავხატე** (*davkhat'e*) → **დავხატო** (*davkhat'o*).

### Verb: To Do / To Make (გაკეთება, *gak'eteba*)
*Aorist Base: გავაკეთე (gavak'ete - I did) / Optative: გავაკეთო (gavak'eto - [that] I do)*

| Pronoun[^12] | Optative Conjugation[^7] | Transliteration | Trigger Example | English Meaning |
|---------|----------------------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| მე (*me*) | **გავაკეთო** | gavak'eto | უნდა **გავაკეთო** (*unda gavak'eto*) | I must do |
| შენ (*shen*) | **გააკეთო** | gaak'eto | მინდა, რომ **გააკეთო** (*minda, rom gaak'eto*) | I want you to do |
| მან (*man*) | **გააკეთოს** | gaak'etos | შეუძლია **გააკეთოს** (*sheudzlia gaak'etos*) | He/She can do |
| ჩვენ (*chven*) | **გავაკეთოთ** | gavak'etot | უნდა **გავაკეთოთ** (*unda gavak'etot*) | We must do |
| თქვენ (*tkven*) | **გააკეთოთ** | gaak'etot | უნდა **გააკეთოთ** (*unda gaak'etot*) | You (pl) must do |
| მათ (*mat*) | **გააკეთონ** | gaak'eton | მინდა, რომ **გააკეთონ** (*minda, rom gaak'eton*) | I want them to do |

### Verb: To Write (დაწერა, *dats'era*)
*Aorist Base: დავწერე (davts'ere - I wrote) / Optative: დავწერო (davts'ero - [that] I write)*

| Pronoun | Optative Conjugation | Transliteration | Trigger Example | English Meaning |
|---------|----------------------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| მე (*me*) | **დავწერო** | davts'ero | შემიძლია **დავწერო** (*shemidzlia davts'ero*) | I can write |
| მან (*man*) | **დაწეროს** | dats'eros | უნდა **დაწეროს** (*unda dats'eros*) | He/She must write |

## 3. The Crucial Irregulars: To Be & To Go

Just like in the past tense[^9], the verbs "To be" and "To go" do their own thing. Notice how "To go" uses **-ე** (*-e*) and **-ეს** (*-es*) on its past stem (`წავიდ-` *ts'avid-*), while "To be" uses **-ო** (*-o*) and **-ოს** (*-os*) on `ყ-` (*q'-*). You must memorize these, as you will use them constantly.

### To Go (წასვლა, *ts'asvla*)
| Pronoun | Optative | Transliteration | Trigger Example | English |
|---------|----------|-----------------|-----------------|---------|
| მე (*me*) | **წავიდე** | ts'avide | უნდა **წავიდე** (*unda ts'avide*) | I must go |
| შენ (*shen*) | **წახვიდე** | ts'akhvide | მინდა, რომ **წახვიდე** (*minda, rom ts'akhvide*) | I want you to go |
| ის (*is*) | **წავიდეს** | ts'avides | მას შეუძლია **წავიდეს** (*mas sheudzlia ts'avides*) | He/She can go |
| ჩვენ (*chven*) | **წავიდეთ** | ts'avidet | უნდა **წავიდეთ** (*unda ts'avidet*) | We must go |

### To Be (ყოფნა, *q'opna*)
| Pronoun | Optative | Transliteration | Trigger Example | English |
|---------|----------|-----------------|-----------------|---------|
| მე (*me*) | **ვიყო** | viq'o | მინდა აქ **ვიყო** (*minda ak viq'o*) | I want to be here |
| შენ (*shen*) | **იყო** | iq'o | უნდა **იყო** კარგად (*unda iq'o k'argad*) | You must be well |
| ის (*is*) | **იყოს** | iq'os | უნდა **იყოს** (*unda iq'os*) | He/She must be |

## 4. The Grammar Trap: The Ergative Case[^1] Returns!

Do you remember the Ergative (`-მა` *-ma* / `-მ` *-m*) case from Module 13? The rule was that in the Past Tense (Aorist[^14]), Transitive[^8] verbs force their subjects into the Ergative case.

Here is the ultimate Georgian grammar secret: **The Optative mood belongs to the same family as the Past Tense.** 

This means that if your Optative verb is a Transitive (action) verb, **the subject[^11] MUST be in the Ergative case!** (Intransitive[^3] verbs like "to go" or "to be" stay in the Nominative).

**Look at the Case Flip in Action:**
*   **Intransitive (Nom):** ნიკა უნდა წავიდეს. (Nik'a unda ts'avides) — Nika must go. *(Nika stays Nom)*
*   **Transitive (Erg):** ნიკა**მ** უნდა დაწეროს წერილ**ი**. (Nik'am unda dats'eros ts'erili) — Nika must write a letter. *(Nika becomes Erg, Letter drops to Nom!)*

> **🗣️ Pro Tip:** The word `უნდა` (*unda*) is frozen when it means "must". It never changes its shape. Just place it before the Optative verb, and let the Optative verb dictate the cases!

---

## 5. "Let's Do It!" (First Person[^5] Plural[^15])

There is one more incredibly useful feature of the Optative. If you use the 1st Person Plural ("We") form of the Optative all by itself, without a trigger word, it translates to **"Let's...!"**

*   **წავიდეთ!** (ts'avidet!) — Let's go!
*   **გავაკეთოთ!** (gavak'etot!) — Let's do it!
*   **შევჭამოთ!** (shevch'amot!) — Let's eat!
*   **დავლიოთ!** (davliot!) — Let's drink!

## 6. Practice Dialogue: Making Plans

Read this dialogue aloud. Notice how the speakers use the Optative to express what they want to do, what they must do, and how they suggest plans ("Let's...").

**სოფო (Sopo):** გიორგი, მინდა, რომ დღეს კინოში **წავიდეთ**. (Giorgi, minda, rom dghes k'inoshi **ts'avidet**. - *Giorgi, I want us **to go** to the cinema today.*)
**გიორგი (Giorgi):** ბოდიში, სოფო. დღეს ვმუშაობ, ამიტომ ბანკში უნდა **ვიყო**. (Bodishi, Sopo. Dghes vmushaob, amit'om bank'shi unda **viq'o**. - *Sorry, Sopo. I am working today, therefore I must **be** at the bank.*)
**სოფო (Sopo):** ხვალ შეგიძლია? (Khval shegidzlia? - *Can you tomorrow?*)
**გიორგი (Giorgi):** კი, ხვალ შემიძლია. რა ფილმი უნდა **ვნახოთ**? (K'i, khval shemidzlia. Ra pilmi unda **vnakhot**? - *Yes, I can tomorrow. What movie should we **see**?*)
**სოფო (Sopo):** ქართული ფილმი ვნახოთ. (Kartuli pilmi vnakhot. - *Let's see a Georgian movie.*)
**გიორგი (Giorgi):** კარგი იდეაა. ხვალ **წავიდეთ**! (K'argi ideaa. Khval **ts'avidet**! - *Good idea. Let's **go** tomorrow!*)

### Next Steps
- Memorize the two most important phrases in Georgia: `უნდა წავიდე` (*unda ts'avide* - I must go) and `წავიდეთ!` (*ts'avidet!* - Let's go!).
- Write down three things you *must* do today using `მე უნდა... [Optative]` (*me unda...*). 
- Remember the trap: If you must *eat* or *write* or *build* something, a 3rd-person subject takes the `-მა` / `-მ` (*-ma* / *-m*) Ergative suffix[^13]!

Now that you can express desires, obligations, and invitations, you are ready to report unseen events and life experiences in **Module 16: The Perfect Tense (Evidentiality)!**

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Ergative case** (მოთხრობითი ბრუნვა, *motkhrobiti brunva*) — A grammatical case used to mark the subject of transitive verbs in certain tenses (like the aorist).
[^2]: **Second person** (მეორე პირი, *meore p'iri*) — A grammatical category referring to the listener.
[^3]: **Intransitive** (გარდაუვალი, *gardauvali*) — A verb that does not take a direct object.
[^4]: **Future tense** (მყოფადი დრო, *mq'opadi dro*) — The tense used for actions that will happen.
[^5]: **First person** (პირველი პირი, *p'irveli p'iri*) — A grammatical category referring to the speaker.
[^6]: **Third person** (მესამე პირი, *mesame p'iri*) — A grammatical category referring to someone other than the speaker or listener.
[^7]: **Conjugation** (უღვლილება, *ughvlileba*) — The process of changing a verb's form to indicate tense, person, or number.
[^8]: **Transitive** (გარდამავალი, *gardamavali*) — A verb that requires a direct object to receive its action.
[^9]: **Past tense** (წარსული დრო, *ts'arsuli dro*) — The tense used for completed actions.
[^10]: **Optative** (კავშირებითი მწკრივი, *k'avshirebiti mts'k'rivi*) — A verb form used to express wishes, possibilities, obligations, or hypothetical situations.
[^11]: **Subject** (ქვემდებარე, *kvemdebare*) — The main person or thing that performs the action or is described in a sentence.
[^12]: **Pronoun** (ნაცვალსახელი, *natsvalsakheli*) — A word that takes the place of a noun.
[^13]: **Suffix** (სუფიქსი / ბოლოსართი, *supiksi* / *bolosarti*) — A grammatical element added to the end of a word.
[^14]: **Aorist** (წყვეტილი მწკრივი, *ts'q'vet'ili mts'k'rivi*) — A specific past tense used for completed, simple actions without focusing on their duration.
[^15]: **Plural** (მრავლობითი რიცხვი, *mravlobiti ritskhvi*) — A grammatical form indicating more than one person or thing.
[^16]: **Verb** (ზმნა, *zmna*) — An action word or word showing a state of being.
