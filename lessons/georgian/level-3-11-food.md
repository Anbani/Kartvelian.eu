Welcome to Module 11! You have officially reached the stage where you can navigate, schedule, and converse in Georgia. Now, it's time to enjoy the absolute best part of Georgian culture: **the food.**

Georgian cuisine is world-renowned, and its centerpiece is the **სუფრა** (*supra*) — the traditional feast, where the food is inseparable from the toasts, the polyphonic singing, and the wine. We'll meet the **სუფრა** (*supra*) properly in Module 18; for now, the goal is simply to expand your culinary vocabulary, learn how to describe how your food tastes, and upgrade your restaurant etiquette so you can order like a polite local rather than a demanding tourist.

## 2. Adjectives of Taste and Temperature

When the waiter asks how the food is, you will want to give a better response than just **კარგია** (*k'argia* — "it is good"). 

Remember the street Georgian trick from Module 3: just attach **-ა** (*-a*, "is") directly to the dictionary form of the adjective[^1] — keep its final vowel — to make a full sentence!

| Adjective (Dictionary) | Georgian (`+ა`, *-a*) | Transliteration | English Meaning |
|------------------------|-----------------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| გემრიელი (*gemrieli*, Delicious) | **გემრიელია** | *gemrielia* | It is delicious! |
| ცხარე (*tskhare*, Spicy) | **ცხარეა** | *tskharea* | It is spicy! |
| ტკბილი (*t'k'bili*, Sweet) | **ტკბილია** | *t'k'bilia* | It is sweet! |
| მჟავე (*mzhave*, Sour) | **მჟავეა** | *mzhavea* | It is sour. |
| ცხელი (*tskheli*, Hot) | **ცხელია** | *tskhelia* | It is hot. |
| ცივი (*tsivi*, Cold) | **ცივია** | *tsivia* | It is cold. |

> **🗣️ Language Hack:** If something is *very* delicious, add **ძალიან** (*dzalian* — "very") in front of it: **ძალიან გემრიელია!** (*Dzalian gemrielia!* — "It is very delicious!")

## 3. Polite Ordering: "If it is possible"

In Module 4, you learned **ეს მინდა** (*es minda* — "I want this"). While grammatically correct, just saying "I want" can sound a bit abrupt in a nice restaurant. Here is how to soften your requests.

### The Magic Phrase: `თუ შეიძლება` (*tu sheidzleba*)
This literally translates to "if it is possible," and it is the standard Georgian way to say **"please"** when requesting something or getting attention. 

*   **მენიუ, თუ შეიძლება.** (*meniu, tu sheidzleba*) — The menu, please.
*   **ანგარიში, თუ შეიძლება.** (*angarishi, tu sheidzleba*) — The bill, please.
*   **ერთი ყავა, თუ შეიძლება.** (*erti q'ava, tu sheidzleba*) — One coffee, please.

### The Polite Command: `მომიტანეთ` (*momit'anet*)
This is a formal command meaning **"Bring me..."**. You use it when giving the waiter your actual order. 

**Formula:** `[Item] + მომიტანეთ, გთხოვთ.` (*[Item] + momit'anet, gtkhovt* — "Bring me [Item], please.")
*   **წყალი მომიტანეთ, გთხოვთ.** (*ts'q'ali momit'anet, gtkhovt*) — Bring me water, please.
*   **ხაჭაპური მომიტანეთ.** (*khach'ap'uri momit'anet*) — Bring me khachapuri.

## 4. The Restaurant Experience: Step-by-Step

Here are the key phrases you will need from the moment you sit down to the moment you leave.

**1. Getting Attention:**
Unlike in many Western countries, waiters in Georgia will often leave you in peace until you signal them. Politely wave or call out:
*   **უკაცრავად!** (*uk'atsravad!*) — Excuse me!

**2. Ordering Portions:**
Remember the Golden Rule from Module 4! Numbers always take singular[^2] nouns. 
*   **ათი ხინკალი** (*ati khink'ali*) — Ten khinkali *(Not khink'alebi!)*
*   **ორი ლუდი** (*ori ludi*) — Two beers.

**3. Leaving a Tip:**
Georgians call a tip **ჩაის ფული** (*chais puli* — literally *"tea money"*), a calque from Russian *чаевые*. Most restaurants in Georgia add a mandatory 10% to 18% service charge to the bill, but it is still customary to leave a few extra Lari in cash for the waiter.

## 5. Practice Dialogue: The Khinkali Order

Read this dialogue aloud. Pay attention to how the guest uses numbers, politely requests items, and asks for the bill.

**მიმტანი** (*mimt'ani*, Waiter): გამარჯობა. (*Gamarjoba.* — *Hello.*)
**სტუმარი** (*st'umari*, Guest): გამარჯობა. მენიუ, თუ შეიძლება. (*Gamarjoba. Meniu, tu sheidzleba.* — *Hello. The menu, please.*)
*[A few minutes later...]*
**მიმტანი** (*mimt'ani*, Waiter): რას შეუკვეთავთ? (*Ras sheuk'vetavt?* — *What will you order?*)
**სტუმარი** (*st'umari*, Guest): ათი ხინკალი, კიტრი-პომიდვრის სალათი და ერთი ლუდი მომიტანეთ, გთხოვთ. (*Ati khink'ali, k'it'ri-p'omidvris salati da erti ludi momit'anet, gtkhovt.* — *Bring me ten khinkali, a cucumber-tomato salad, and one beer, please.*)
**მიმტანი** (*mimt'ani*, Waiter): დიდი ხინკალი გინდათ? (*Didi khink'ali gindat?* — *Do you want large khinkali?*)
**სტუმარი** (*st'umari*, Guest): კი, დიდი. მადლობა. (*K'i, didi. Madloba.* — *Yes, large. Thank you.*)
*[After the meal...]*
**სტუმარი** (*st'umari*, Guest): უკაცრავად! ანგარიში, თუ შეიძლება. ძალიან გემრიელია! (*Uk'atsravad! Angarishi, tu sheidzleba. Dzalian gemrielia!* — *Excuse me! The bill, please. It is very delicious!*)

> **🥟 Cultural Rule:** NEVER eat **ხინკალი** (*khink'ali*, Georgian soup dumplings) with a knife and fork. You must pick them up by the doughy top knot or "tail" (**კუდი** — *k'udi*), bite a small hole, drink the hot broth, and then eat the rest of the dumpling. Leave the **კუდი** (*k'udi*) tops on your plate so you can count how many you ate!
