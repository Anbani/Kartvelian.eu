Welcome to Module 18! You have reached a level of Georgian where grammar is no longer your biggest barrier—culture is. 

To truly connect with Georgians, you must understand the unwritten rules of their society. Language and culture in Georgia are inseparable. In this module, we will explore the heart of Georgian social life (the *Supra*), learn the slang that textbooks refuse to teach, and take a quick tour of the country's colorful regional dialects.

## 1. The Supra (სუფრა, *supra*): The Georgian Feast

The word **სუფრა** (supra) literally translates to "tablecloth," but it represents the cornerstone of Georgian social life. It is a highly structured feast involving endless food, polyphonic singing, and, most importantly, wine. 

To survive a supra, you must know the key players and rules:

*   **თამადა (Tamada):** The Toastmaster. The supreme leader of the table. He dictates the flow of the evening. **You cannot drink your wine until the Tamada has made a toast.**
*   **მერიქიფე (Merikipe):** The Cupbearer. The person responsible for ensuring no one's glass is ever empty. (If you want to stop drinking, leave your glass half-full!).
*   **ალავერდი (Alaverdi):** The Pass. The Tamada cannot speak forever. He will often declare a toast, drink, and then say "Alaverdi [Name]!" This means he is passing the floor to that specific person to elaborate on the same theme.

## 2. Street Georgian: Slang & Expressions

Textbook Georgian is beautiful, but if you want to sound like a local, you need to pepper your speech with these untranslatable cultural gems.

### Terms of Endearment
*   **გენაცვალე (Genatsvale):** The ultimate Georgian word. It literally translates to "May I replace you (in hardship)"—similar in spirit to **შენი ჭირიმე** (*sheni ch'irime*, "May I take your pain/sorrow"). It is used constantly to show deep affection, gratitude, or warmth. 
*   **ჯიგარი (Jigari):** Literally translates to "internal organs" (liver/heart). If you call someone *jigari* (or **ჯიგარო** *jigaro* in the vocative when addressing them), it means they are a top-tier, solid, authentic, and generous person. 

### Filler Words & Slang
*   **რავი / რავიცი (Ravi / Ravitsi):** Short for "I don't know" (**რა ვიცი**, *ra vitsi*). It is the Georgian equivalent of saying "well..." or "um..." at the start of a sentence.
*   **მაგარია (Magaria):** Literally means "it is hard/firm," but in slang, it means **"It is awesome/cool."**
    *   *Example:* ეგ კაცი ძალიან მაგარია! (*Eg k'atsi dzalian magaria!* - That man is very cool!)
*   **კარგი რა (K'argi ra):** Literally "Good what," but used to mean **"Oh, come on!"** or "Give me a break!" Use it when a friend is exaggerating.

## 3. Regional Accents & Stereotypes

Georgia is a small country, but it is deeply mountainous, meaning regional identities and dialects are incredibly strong. Georgians love to playfully stereotype each other based on where they are from.

### The Gurians (გურულები, *gurulebi*)
From the western region of Guria. They are famous for speaking at lightning speed. If you think standard Georgian is fast, Gurian sounds like a machine gun. They are stereotyped as witty, impatient, and constantly moving.

### The Kakhetians (კახელები, *k'akhelebi*)
From the eastern wine region of Kakheti. They are the exact opposite of Gurians. They speak slowly, heavily elongating their vowels, and are stereotyped as straightforward, hardworking, and deeply serious about their vineyards.
*   *Kakhetian trait:* Adding an extended **"ჯო" (jo)** to the end of words or sentences for emphasis.

### The Megrelians (მეგრელები, *megrelebi*)
From the western region of Samegrelo. Megrelians actually have their own distinct Kartvelian language (Megrelian) which is unintelligible to standard Georgian speakers. However, when they speak standard Georgian, they have a sing-song, melodic accent and use the word **და (da - and)** constantly at the end of their sentences. They are stereotyped as hospitable, quick-witted, and makers of the spiciest food in the country.

## 4. The Golden Rule: Guest Culture

In Georgia, there is a famous proverb: **"სტუმარი ღვთისაა"** (st'umari ghvtisaa) — *A guest is from God.*

If you are invited into a Georgian home, you will be treated like royalty. It is culturally unacceptable for a guest to pay for a meal at a restaurant if they were invited by a Georgian. Conversely, if *you* invite a Georgian out, be prepared to sneak away to the waiter to pay the bill, because they will fight you for it!

### Putting It All Together: A Toast to You

Imagine you are at a Supra. The Tamada raises his glass of amber wine, looks directly at you, and says:

> **"ჩვენს სტუმარს გაუმარჯოს! საქართველოში ჩამოვიდა, ქართულს სწავლობს... ჯიგარი კაცია! გენაცვალე!"**
> 
> *(Chvens st'umars gaumarjos! Sakartveloshi chamovida, kartuls sts'avlobs... jigari k'atsia! Genatsvale!)*
> 
> *"Victory to our guest! He came to Georgia, he is learning Georgian... he is a solid guy! Bless you!"*

Your only job now is to smile, raise your glass, and shout with the rest of the table: **"გაუმარჯოს!"** (*Gaumarjos!*)
