Welcome to Module 6! Now that you have the basics down, it is time to unlock the real engine of the Georgian language: **Cases and Verbs**.

In English, we rely heavily on word order to know who is doing what to whom. Georgian, however, relies on cases—little suffixes attached to the end of nouns. The most important case you will learn after the Nominative (dictionary form) is the **Dative Case[^3]**. 

In this module, you will learn how to form the Dative case and use it with Georgian's famously quirky "Inversion Verbs" in the present tense[^2] to express what you want and what you have.

---

## 1. Forming the Dative Case (`-ს`, *-s*)

The Dative case is primarily used for the **indirect object[^1]** of a sentence (the person or thing receiving the action, like saying "I gave it *to the dog*"). 

Forming the Dative case is straightforward. The marker is the letter **-ს** (*-s*).

### Rule A: Words ending in a vowel[^8] (`-ა, -ე, -ო, -უ` — *-a, -e, -o, -u*)
Simply attach **-ს** (*-s*) to the end of the word.

| Nominative (Dictionary) | Dative Form (`+ს`, *-s*) | Transliteration | English |
|-------------------------|--------------------------|-----------------|---------|
| დედა (*deda*, Mother) | **დედას** | *dedas* | To the mother |
| გოგო (*gogo*, Girl) | **გოგოს** | *gogos* | To the girl |
| ხე (*khe*, Tree) | **ხეს** | *khes* | To the tree |

### Rule B: Common nouns ending in `-ი` (*-i*)
If a noun[^10] ends in the standard Nominative **-ი** (*-i*), you **drop the `-ი` (*-i*)** and replace it with **-ს** (*-s*). 

| Nominative (Dictionary) | Drop `-ი` (*-i*), Add `-ს` (*-s*) | Transliteration | English |
|-------------------------|-----------------------------------|-----------------|---------|
| ძაღლი (*dzaghli*, Dog) | **ძაღლს** | *dzaghls* | To the dog |
| მეგობარი (*megobari*, Friend) | **მეგობარს** | *megobars* | To the friend |
| წიგნი (*ts'igni*, Book) | **წიგნს** | *ts'igns* | To the book |

### Rule C: Proper names
Personal names ending in a vowel (**-ა, -ე, -ო, -უ**) or whose root ends in **-ი** (like **გიორგი**, *Giorgi*) **keep their final vowel and add `-ს` (*-s*)** (while consonant-stem names ending in nominative `-ი`, such as **დავითი** *Daviti* or **თამარი** *Tamari*, drop `-ი`: **დავითს** *Davits*, **თამარს** *Tamars*).

| Name | Keep vowel, Add `-ს` (*-s*) | Transliteration | English |
|------|-----------------------------|-----------------|---------|
| ანა (*Ana*) | **ანას** | *Anas* | To Ana |
| გიორგი (*Giorgi*) | **გიორგის** | *Giorgis* | To Giorgi |
| ნიკა (*Nik'a*) | **ნიკას** | *Nik'as* | To Nika |
| ელენე (*Elene*) | **ელენეს** | *Elenes* | To Elene |

## 2. Personal Pronouns in the Dative

Before we tackle the verbs, you need to know how to say "to me", "to you", and "to him/her". 

Here is the best news you will get all week: **The 1st- and 2nd-person pronouns do not change!** "I" and "to me" are the exact same word (**მე**, *me*). Only the 3rd-person pronouns (he/she/it/they) change.

| Person | Nominative (I, You, He) | Dative (To me, To you, To him) |
|--------|-------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **1st (Singular[^4])** | მე (*me*) | **მე** (*me*) |
| **2nd (Singular)** | შენ (*shen*) | **შენ** (*shen*) |
| **3rd (Singular)** | ის (*is*) | **მას** (*mas*) |
| **1st (Plural[^7])** | ჩვენ (*chven*) | **ჩვენ** (*chven*) |
| **2nd (Plural)** | თქვენ (*tkven*) | **თქვენ** (*tkven*) |
| **3rd (Plural)** | ისინი (*isini*) | **მათ** (*mat*) |

## 3. Inversion Verbs: The "To Me Is" Concept

In English, when you say "I want an apple", **"I"** is the subject[^5] doing the action.
In Georgian, verbs that express feelings, wants, or possession are called **Inversion Verbs**. The logic is flipped. Georgians literally say: **"To me is wanted an apple."**

Because the sentence is structured as "To me...", the person who *wants* or *has* something must be in the **Dative Case**, while the thing wanted or owned stays in the **Nominative Case**.

### The Verb[^9]: To Want (**მინდა**, *minda*)
Notice how the core of the word stays the same, while the prefixes (and plural suffix **-თ**, *-t*) change to match the person.

| Pronoun[^6] (Dative) | Verb Form | Transliteration | Literal Meaning | English Translation |
|----------------------|-----------|-----------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| მე (*me*) | **მინდა** | *minda* | To me is wanted | I want |
| შენ (*shen*) | **გინდა** | *ginda* | To you is wanted | You want |
| მას (*mas*) | **უნდა** | *unda* | To him/her is wanted | He/She wants |
| ჩვენ (*chven*) | **გვინდა** | *gvinda* | To us is wanted | We want |
| თქვენ (*tkven*) | **გინდათ** | *gindat* | To you (pl.) is wanted | You (pl./formal) want |
| მათ (*mat*) | **უნდათ** | *undat* | To them is wanted | They want |

**Examples:**
*   **მე მინდა წყალი.** (*me minda ts'q'ali*) — I want water.
*   **მას უნდა ყავა.** (*mas unda q'ava*) — He/She wants coffee.
*   **გიორგის უნდა პური.** (*Giorgis unda p'uri*) — Giorgi wants bread. *(Notice **გიორგის**, *Giorgis*, is in the Dative case!)*

## 4. The Verb "To Have" (The Inanimate / Animate Split)

Georgian has another essential distinction: there are **two different verbs** for "to have", depending on whether the thing you possess is inanimate or animate. Mixing these up sounds very strange to a Georgian!

Like "to want", these are Inversion Verbs. The owner is in the Dative case ("To me is had...").

### A. To Have... an Object (Inanimate: **მაქვს**, *makvs*)
Use this for inanimate objects and abstract things: money, houses, books, phones, time, ideas.

*   მე **მაქვს** (*me makvs*) — I have...
*   შენ **გაქვს** (*shen gakvs*) — You have...
*   მას **აქვს** (*mas akvs*) — He/She has...
*   ჩვენ **გვაქვს** (*chven gvakvs*) — We have...
*   თქვენ **გაქვთ** (*tkven gakvt*) — You (pl./formal) have...
*   მათ **აქვთ** (*mat akvt*) — They have...

**Example:** **მე მაქვს სახლი.** (*me makvs sakhli*) — I have a house.

### B. To Have... a Living Being (Animate: **მყავს**, *mq'avs*)
Use this for humans and animals (family members, friends, pets)—**as well as wheeled/moving vehicles** (such as a car, **მანქანა** *mankana*, or bicycle, **ველოსიპედი** *velosip'edi*)!

*   მე **მყავს** (*me mq'avs*) — I have...
*   შენ **გყავს** (*shen gq'avs*) — You have...
*   მას **ჰყავს** (*mas hq'avs*) — He/She has...
*   ჩვენ **გვყავს** (*chven gvq'avs*) — We have...
*   თქვენ **გყავთ** (*tkven gq'avt*) — You (pl./formal) have...
*   მათ **ჰყავთ** (*mat hq'avt*) — They have...

**Examples:**
*   **მე მყავს ძაღლი.** (*me mq'avs dzaghli*) — I have a dog.
*   **მე მყავს მანქანა.** (*me mq'avs mankana*) — I have a car. *(Vehicles are treated like mounts/living beings in Georgian!)*

> **🚨 Error Warning:** If you say **მე მაქვს ძაღლი** (*me makvs dzaghli*, using the inanimate verb for a dog), you are grammatically treating your dog like a piece of furniture. Always use **მყავს** (*mq'avs*) for living beings (and vehicles)!

## 5. Practice and Application

Let's put the Dative case and our inversion verbs together. Read these sentences out loud and analyze why each word is in its specific case.

**1. ანას უნდა ჩაი.** (*Anas unda chai.*)
*   *Translation:* Ana wants tea.
*   *Why?* **ანა** (*Ana*) gets the **-ს** (*-s*) marker because "want" is an inversion verb ("To Ana is wanted tea").

**2. შენ გაქვს ლარი?** (*Shen gakvs lari?*)
*   *Translation:* Do you have a Lari?
*   *Why?* We use **გაქვს** (*gakvs*) because money is an inanimate object.

**3. მას ჰყავს კატა.** (*Mas hq'avs k'at'a.*)
*   *Translation:* He/She has a cat.
*   *Why?* We use the 3rd-person Dative pronoun **მას** (*mas*) and the animate verb **ჰყავს** (*hq'avs*) because a cat is a living being.

**4. მეგობარს უნდა სახლი.** (*Megobars unda sakhli.*)
*   *Translation:* The friend wants a house.
*   *Why?* **მეგობარი** (*megobari*) drops the `-ი` (*-i*) and takes `-ს` (*-s*) to become **მეგობარს** (*megobars*).

### Next Steps
- Write down 3 inanimate things you have using **მე მაქვს...** (*me makvs...*).
- Write down 3 living beings (or vehicles) you have using **მე მყავს...** (*me mq'avs...*).
- Ask a Georgian friend (or practice out loud): **შენ რა გინდა?** (*shen ra ginda?* — "What do you want?")

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Indirect object** (ირიბი დამატება, *iribi damat'eba*) — The noun that is the recipient, experiencer, or beneficiary of the action.
[^2]: **Present tense** (ახლანდელი დრო, *akhlandeli dro*) — The tense used for actions or states happening right now or habitually.
[^3]: **Dative case** (მიცემითი ბრუნვა, *mitsemiti brunva*) — A grammatical case used for indirect objects, or subjects with certain experiential/inversion verbs.
[^4]: **Singular** (მხოლობითი რიცხვი, *mkholobiti ritskhvi*) — A grammatical form indicating one person or thing.
[^5]: **Subject** (ქვემდებარე, *kvemdebare*) — The main person or thing that performs the action or is described in a sentence.
[^6]: **Pronoun** (ნაცვალსახელი, *natsvalsakheli*) — A word that takes the place of a noun.
[^7]: **Plural** (მრავლობითი რიცხვი, *mravlobiti ritskhvi*) — A grammatical form indicating more than one person or thing.
[^8]: **Vowel** (ხმოვანი, *khmovani*) — A speech sound made with your mouth open, not blocked by your teeth, tongue, or lips.
[^9]: **Verb** (ზმნა, *zmna*) — An action word or word showing a state of being.
[^10]: **Noun** (არსებითი სახელი, *arsebiti sakheli*) — A word that represents a person, place, thing, or idea.
