Welcome to Module 3! If you've ever struggled with languages that assign a gender to every single object—memorizing whether a table is masculine or a chair is feminine—you are going to love Georgian. 

Georgian has **zero grammatical gender[^1]**. A house is just a house, a dog is just a dog, and there are **no articles** (no "a", "an", or "the"). 

In this lesson, we will cover how nouns work in their dictionary form, how to turn them into plurals, and how to point things out using demonstratives ("this" and "that").

---

## 1. The Dictionary Form (The Nominative Case[^3])

Whenever you look up a noun[^11] in a Georgian dictionary, you will find it in the Nominative case. This case is used for the main subject[^7] of a sentence.

Almost all Georgian nouns end in a vowel[^9]. 
*   If the core word (the stem[^12]) naturally ends in a consonant[^5], Georgian adds an **-ი** (*-i*) to the end.
*   If the core word already ends in a vowel (**-ა, -ე, -ო, -უ** — *-a, -e, -o, -u*), it just stays that way.

| Noun Ending | Georgian | Transliteration | English |
|-------------|----------|-----------------|---------|
| Ends in **-ი** (*-i*) | სახლ**ი** | *sakhl***i** | House / The house |
| Ends in **-ი** (*-i*) | ძაღლ**ი** | *dzaghl***i** | Dog / The dog |
| Ends in **-ა** (*-a*) | კატ**ა** | *k'at'***a** | Cat / The cat |
| Ends in **-ე** (*-e*) | ხ**ე** | *kh***e** | Tree / The tree |
| Ends in **-ო** (*-o*) | გოგ**ო** | *gog***o** | Girl / The girl |
| Ends in **-უ** (*-u*) | ბ**უ** | *b***u** | Owl / The owl |

> **🗣️ Language Hack:** Because there are no articles in Georgian, **წყალი** (*ts'q'ali*) can translate to "water", "a water", or "the water" depending entirely on the context of your conversation.

## 2. Making Plurals: The Magic of `-ებ-` (*-eb-*)

To make a noun plural[^8] in English, we usually add "-s". In Georgian, you use the plural marker **-ებ-** (*-eb-*), which is followed by the nominative vowel **-ი** (*-i*), producing the plural ending **-ები** (*-ebi*).

The rules for adding **-ები** (*-ebi*) depend on the noun's final vowel:

### Rule A: Words ending in `-ი` (*-i*) or `-ა` (*-a*) drop their final vowel.
If the dictionary form ends in **-ი** (*-i*) or **-ა** (*-a*), remove that vowel and add **-ები** (*-ebi*).

| Singular[^6] | Drop the Vowel | Add `-ები` (*-ebi*, Plural) | Transliteration | English |
|--------------|----------------|-----------------------------|-----------------|---------|
| სახლ**ი** (*sakhli*, House) | სახლ- (*sakhl-*) | **სახლები** | *sakhlebi* | Houses |
| ძაღლ**ი** (*dzaghli*, Dog) | ძაღლ- (*dzaghl-*) | **ძაღლები** | *dzaghlebi* | Dogs |
| კატ**ა** (*k'at'a*, Cat) | კატ- (*k'at'-*) | **კატები** | *k'at'ebi* | Cats |
| მანქან**ა** (*mankana*, Car) | მანქან- (*mankan-*) | **მანქანები** | *mankanebi* | Cars |

### Rule B: Words ending in `-ე` (*-e*), `-ო` (*-o*), or `-უ` (*-u*) keep their vowel.
If the dictionary form ends in **-ე** (*-e*), **-ო** (*-o*), or **-უ** (*-u*), keep the vowel and attach **-ები** (*-ebi*) directly after it. (Remember Module 1: pronounce both adjacent vowels clearly!)

| Singular | Keep the Vowel | Add `-ები` (*-ebi*, Plural) | Transliteration | English |
|----------|----------------|-----------------------------|-----------------|---------|
| ხ**ე** (*khe*, Tree) | ხე- (*khe-*) | **ხეები** | *kheebi* | Trees |
| გოგ**ო** (*gogo*, Girl) | გოგო- (*gogo-*) | **გოგოები** | *gogoebi* | Girls |
| ბ**უ** (*bu*, Owl) | ბუ- (*bu-*) | **ბუები** | *buebi* | Owls |

## 3. Demonstratives: This and That

Now that you have nouns, you need to be able to point at things. Georgian uses two primary demonstrative pronouns for beginners:

*   **ეს** (*es*) — This (close to you)
*   **ის** (*is*) — That (far from you, or out of reach)

To make a basic sentence, you just need the verb[^10] "to be" for the third person[^4] singular: **არის** (*aris* — "is").

*   **ეს არის სახლი.** (*es aris sakhli*) — This is a house.
*   **ის არის ხე.** (*is aris khe*) — That is a tree.
*   **ეს არის ჩემი ძაღლი.** (*es aris chemi dzaghli*) — This is my dog.

### The Plural Demonstratives (These & Those)
When standing alone as pronouns referring to multiple things (especially people), the demonstratives have plural forms:
*   **ესენი** (*eseni*) — These (ones)
*   **ისინი** (*isini*) — Those (ones) / They

However, when introducing plural nouns ("These are... / Those are..."), Georgian routinely keeps the pointer singular (**ეს** *es* / **ის** *is*), and for **inanimate plural nouns** the verb also remains singular (**არის**, *aris*):

*   **ეს არის მანქანები.** (*es aris mankanebi*) — These are cars. *(Literally: "This is cars.")*
*   **ის არის სახლები.** (*is aris sakhlebi*) — Those are houses. *(Literally: "That is houses.")*

> **🧠 Why the verb stays singular:** In Georgian grammar, inanimate plural nouns in **-ები** (*-ebi*) (like **მანქანები** *mankanebi* and **სახლები** *sakhlebi*) regularly take a **singular** verb (**არის**, *aris*), whereas plural nouns referring to people take the plural verb **არიან** (*arian*, "are") with a plural predicate nominal[^2] (e.g., **ესენი არიან სტუდენტები**, *eseni arian st'udent'ebi* — "These are students").

## 4. How Georgians Actually Speak: Dropping "არის" (*aris*)

In a textbook, you will see `ეს არის კატა` (*es aris k'at'a*, "This is a cat"). But on the streets of Tbilisi, Georgians speak fast. They almost always drop the full word **არის** (*aris*) and replace it with a short enclitic **-ა** (*-a*) attached to the end of the noun.

**Textbook vs. Street Georgian:**
*   ეს **არის** სახლ**ი** (*es aris sakhli*) → ეს სახლი**ა** (*es sakhlia* — This is a house).
*   ის **არის** მანქან**ა** (*is aris mankana*) → ის მანქანა**ა** (*is mankanaa* — That is a car).
*   ეს **არის** ჩემი ძაღლ**ი** (*es aris chemi dzaghli*) → ეს ჩემი ძაღლი**ა** (*es chemi dzaghlia* — This is my dog).

> **⚠️ Note on Double Vowels:** If the noun ends in `-ა` (*-a*), attaching the short `-ა` (*-a*) means you will see two `ა`s together (e.g., **მანქანაა**, *mankanaa*). Pronounce both vowels clearly as a double /ɑ/: `man-ka-na-a`.

## Putting It All Together: A Quick Quiz

Test your knowledge by translating these phrases in your head before looking at the answers:

1.  This is a dog.
2.  Those are cats. (Use the conversational singular verb pattern!)
3.  This is my car. (Use the street Georgian `-ა` shortcut!)
4.  That is a girl.

**Answers:**
1. *ეს არის ძაღლი.* (*es aris dzaghli*)
2. *ის არის კატები.* (*is aris k'at'ebi*)
3. *ეს ჩემი მანქანაა.* (*es chemi mankanaa*)
4. *ის არის გოგო.* (*is aris gogo*)

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Grammatical gender** (გრამატიკული სქესი, *gramat'ik'uli skesi*) — A system of noun classification (e.g., masculine, feminine), which is absent in Georgian.
[^2]: **Predicate nominal** (შედგენილი შემასმენლის სახელადი ნაწილი, *shedgenili shemasmenlis sakheladi nats'ili*) — A noun following a linking verb that renames or describes the subject.
[^3]: **Nominative case** (სახელობითი ბრუნვა, *sakhelobiti brunva*) — The basic, dictionary form of a noun, typically used to mark the subject of a sentence.
[^4]: **Third person** (მესამე პირი, *mesame p'iri*) — A grammatical category referring to someone other than the speaker or listener.
[^5]: **Consonant** (თანხმოვანი, *tankhmovani*) — A speech sound that is not a vowel.
[^6]: **Singular** (მხოლობითი რიცხვი, *mkholobiti ritskhvi*) — A grammatical form indicating one person or thing.
[^7]: **Subject** (ქვემდებარე, *kvemdebare*) — The main person or thing that performs the action or is described in a sentence.
[^8]: **Plural** (მრავლობითი რიცხვი, *mravlobiti ritskhvi*) — A grammatical form indicating more than one person or thing.
[^9]: **Vowel** (ხმოვანი, *khmovani*) — A speech sound made with your mouth open, not blocked by your teeth, tongue, or lips.
[^10]: **Verb** (ზმნა, *zmna*) — An action word or word showing a state of being.
[^11]: **Noun** (არსებითი სახელი, *arsebiti sakheli*) — A word that represents a person, place, thing, or idea.
[^12]: **Stem** (ფუძე, *pudze*) — The core, basic part of a word before endings are attached.
