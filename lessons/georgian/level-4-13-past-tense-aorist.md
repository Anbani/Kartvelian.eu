Welcome to Module 13! You have reached the summit. This is the single most famous, infamous, and rewarding milestone in Georgian grammar. 

In this module, we are learning the **Aorist[^14]** (the simple, completed past tense[^8], like "I ate" or "She wrote"). But to use the Aorist, you must master the "Georgian Case Shift" and unlock a brand new case: **The Ergative** (also called the Narrative) case. 

Take a deep breath. It requires a mental flip, but once it clicks, you will truly sound like a Georgian.

---

## 1. The Ergative Case[^1] (`-მა` *-ma* / `-მ` *-m*)

In English, "I" is always the subject[^10], whether I am *eating* an apple or *sleeping* in a bed. 
In Georgian, the rules change in the past tense. If you perform a completed action *to* an object (a Transitive[^7] verb[^16]), the subject must be placed into the **Ergative Case**. 

The Ergative case is marked by the suffix[^13] **-მა (-ma)** or **-მ (-m)**.

### The Rule for Nouns:
1.  **Ends in a consonant[^9] + `-ი` (*-i* in the dictionary form):** Drop the `-ი` (*-i*) and add **-მა** (*-ma*).
    *   ძაღლი (*dzaghli* - Dog) ➔ **ძაღლმა** (dzaghlma)
    *   კაცი (*k'atsi* - Man) ➔ **კაცმა** (k'atsma)
2.  **Ends in a vowel[^15] (`-ა, -ე, -ო, -უ` *-a, -e, -o, -u*):** Keep the vowel and add **-მ** (*-m*).
    *   დედა (*deda* - Mother) ➔ **დედამ** (dedam)
    *   ნიკა (*Nik'a* - Nika) ➔ **ნიკამ** (Nik'am)
    *   **Note on given names:** In vowel-stem names like **გიორგი** (*Giorgi*), the `-ი` (*-i*) belongs to the stem itself, so it stays: **გიორგიმ** (*Giorgim*). Consonant-stem names like **დავით(ი)** (*Davit(i)*) and **თამარ(ი)** (*Tamar(i)*) follow rule 1: **დავითმა** (*Davitma*), **თამარმა** (*Tamarma*).

## 2. The Pronouns in the Past

Here is the best news you will receive all week: **The 1st and 2nd person pronouns do not change!** "I" (მე, *me*) and "You" (შენ, *shen*) are immune to the Ergative case. Only the 3rd person pronouns change.

| Person | Nominative (Present Tense[^3]) | Ergative (Past Tense) |
|--------|----------------------------|-----------------------|
| **1st** | მე (me) | **მე** (me) |
| **2nd** | შენ (shen) | **შენ** (shen) |
| **3rd** | ის (is) | **მან** (man - *He/She/It did*) |
| **1st Pl.**| ჩვენ (chven) | **ჩვენ** (chven) |
| **2nd Pl.**| თქვენ (tkven) | **თქვენ** (tkven) |
| **3rd Pl.**| ისინი (isini) | **მათ** (mat - *They did*) |

---

## 3. The Great Case Flip (Subject vs. Object)

This is the core of Georgian grammar. When you move a transitive verb from the Present to the Past, the cases of the Subject and the Object completely swap places.

**In the Present Tense:**
*   The Subject is in the **Nominative** (`-ი` *-i*).
*   The Object is in the **Dative** (`-ს` *-s*).

**In the Past Tense (Aorist):**
*   The Subject shifts to the **Ergative** (`-მა` *-ma* / `-მ` *-m*).
*   The Object drops back to the **Nominative** (`-ი` *-i*).

Let's look at this side-by-side using the sentence "Nika eats/ate an apple."

| Tense | Subject (Who?) | Verb | Object (What?) | Full Sentence |
|-------|----------------|------|----------------|---------------|
| **Present** | ნიკა (*Nik'a* - Nom) | ჭამს (*ch'ams*) | ვაშლ**ს** (*vashls* - Dat) | ნიკა ჭამს ვაშლს. (Nik'a ch'ams vashls.) |
| **Past** | ნიკა**მ** (*Nik'am* - Erg)| შეჭამა (*shech'ama*) | ვაშლ**ი** (*vashli* - Nom) | ნიკამ შეჭამა ვაშლი. (Nik'am shech'ama vashli.) |

## 4. Conjugating the Aorist (Transitive Verbs)

To form the past tense of an action verb, you need the **Preverb[^12]** (which you learned in Module 12 for the future tense[^5]). Then, the verb endings change. Many regular transitive verbs take `-ე` (*-e*) for "I/You (sg.)", `-ეთ` (*-et*) for "We/You (pl.)", `-ა` (*-a*) for "He/She/It", and `-ეს` (*-es*) for "They".

### Verb: To Do / To Make (`გა-` *ga-* preverb)
*Compare this to the future tense (`გავაკეთებ` gavak'eteb). The `-ებ` (-eb) suffix drops off entirely!*

| Pronoun[^11] | Aorist Conjugation[^6] | Transliteration | English |
|---------|--------------------|-----------------|---------|
| მე (*me*) | **გავაკეთე** | gavak'ete | I did / I made |
| შენ (*shen*) | **გააკეთე** | gaak'ete | You did |
| მან (*man* - Erg) | **გააკეთა** | gaak'eta | He/She did |
| ჩვენ (*chven*) | **გავაკეთეთ** | gavak'etet | We did |
| თქვენ (*tkven*) | **გააკეთეთ** | gaak'etet | You (pl) did |
| მათ (*mat* - Erg) | **გააკეთეს** | gaak'etes | They did |

### Verb: To Write (`და-` *da-* preverb)
| Pronoun | Aorist Conjugation | Transliteration | English |
|---------|--------------------|-----------------|---------|
| მე (*me*) | **დავწერე** | davts'ere | I wrote |
| შენ (*shen*) | **დაწერე** | dats'ere | You wrote |
| მან (*man* - Erg) | **დაწერა** | dats'era | He/She wrote |

**Example Sentence:** 
*   **მან** დაწერა წერილ**ი**. (man dats'era ts'erili) — He/She wrote a letter. *(Notice the Ergative `მან` man and the Nominative object `წერილი` ts'erili!)*

## 5. The Exceptions: Motion & State Verbs

Remember the Transitive vs. Intransitive[^4] split from Module 9? 
**Verbs of motion (going, coming) and state (being) DO NOT use the Ergative case** in the past — their subjects stay in the regular Nominative. (A handful of other intransitive verbs — like *to rest* or *to work* — actually do trigger the Ergative, but for now the rule of thumb is: motion + "to be" stay Nominative; everything else with a direct object[^2] goes Ergative.)

You must memorize the past tense of these two vital irregular verbs:

### 1. To Go (წასვლა, *ts'asvla*) - *Subject stays Nominative!*
| Pronoun | Aorist (Past) | Transliteration | English |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|---------|
| მე (*me*) | **წავედი** | ts'avedi | I went |
| შენ (*shen*) | **წახვედი** | ts'akhvedi | You went |
| ის (*is* - *Nom*) | **წავიდა** | ts'avida | He/She went |
| ჩვენ (*chven*) | **წავედით** | ts'avedit | We went |

### 2. To Be (ყოფნა, *q'opna*) - *Subject stays Nominative!*
| Pronoun | Aorist (Past) | Transliteration | English |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|---------|
| მე (*me*) | **ვიყავი** | viq'avi | I was |
| შენ (*shen*) | **იყავი** | iq'avi | You were |
| ის (*is* - *Nom*) | **იყო** | iq'o | He/She was |
| ჩვენ (*chven*) | **ვიყავით** | viq'avit | We were |

**Example Sentences:**
*   გუშინ მე **წავედი** ბანკში. (gushin me ts'avedi bank'shi) — Yesterday I went to the bank. *(No Ergative needed!)*
*   ის **იყო** სახლში. (is iq'o sakhlshi) — He/She was at home.

## 6. Putting It Together: "What did you do yesterday?"

Read this dialogue carefully. Identify the transitive verbs (which trigger the Ergative case flip) and the intransitive verbs (which don't).

**გიორგი (Giorgi):** გუშინ სად **იყავი**? (Gushin sad **iq'avi**? - *Where **were** you yesterday?*)
**ანა (Ana):** გუშინ დილით სამსახურში **წავედი**. (Gushin dilit samsakhurshi **ts'avedi**. - *Yesterday morning I **went** to work.*)
**გიორგი (Giorgi):** მერე რა **გააკეთე**? (Mere ra **gaak'ete**? - *What **did you do** then?*)
**ანა (Ana):** მერე რესტორანში **წავედი**. მე და ჩემ**მა** მეგობარ**მა** ხინკალ**ი** **შევჭამეთ**. (Mere rest'oranshi **ts'avedi**. Me da chem**ma** megobar**ma** khink'al**i** **shevch'amet**. - *Then I **went** to a restaurant. My friend and I **ate** khinkali.*)
**გიორგი (Giorgi):** ძალიან კარგი. ნიკა სად **იყო**? (Dzalian k'argi. Nik'a sad **iq'o**? - *Very good. Where **was** Nika?*)
**ანა (Ana):** ნიკა**მ** სახლში დაისვენა და წიგნ**ი** **წაიკითხა**. (Nik'a**m** sakhlshi daisvena da ts'ign**i** **ts'aik'itkha**. - *Nika rested at home and **read** a book.*)

*(Notice: Nika read a book -> Transitive -> Nik'a**m** (Erg) ts'ign**i** (Nom) ts'aik'itkha!)*

### Next Steps
- Memorize the 3rd person past pronouns: **მან** (*man* - He/She did) and **მათ** (*mat* - They did).
- Memorize the past tense of "to go" (**წავედი** *ts'avedi*, **წავიდა** *ts'avida*) and "to be" (**ვიყავი** *viq'avi*, **იყო** *iq'o*).
- Write down one sentence about what *you* did yesterday, and one sentence about what *a friend* did yesterday. Make sure to use the `-მა/-მ` (*-ma/-m*) suffix on your friend's name if they performed an action!

Congratulations! You have conquered the Ergative case. Take a moment to celebrate; the rest of the language opens up from here. Next, we will put your Georgian to practical everyday use in **Module 14: Daily Transactions & Services!**

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Ergative case** (მოთხრობითი ბრუნვა, *motkhrobiti brunva*) — A grammatical case used to mark the subject of transitive verbs in certain tenses (like the aorist).
[^2]: **Direct object** (პირდაპირი დამატება, *p'irdap'iri damat'eba*) — The noun that directly receives the action of a transitive verb.
[^3]: **Present tense** (ახლანდელი დრო, *akhlandeli dro*) — The tense used for actions happening right now or habitually.
[^4]: **Intransitive** (გარდაუვალი, *gardauvali*) — A verb that does not take a direct object.
[^5]: **Future tense** (მყოფადი დრო, *mq'opadi dro*) — The tense used for actions that will happen.
[^6]: **Conjugation** (უღვლილება, *ughvlileba*) — The process of changing a verb's form to indicate tense, person, or number.
[^7]: **Transitive** (გარდამავალი, *gardamavali*) — A verb that requires a direct object to receive its action.
[^8]: **Past tense** (წარსული დრო, *ts'arsuli dro*) — The tense used for completed actions.
[^9]: **Consonant** (თანხმოვანი, *tankhmovani*) — A speech sound that is not a vowel.
[^10]: **Subject** (ქვემდებარე, *kvemdebare*) — The main person or thing that performs the action or is described in a sentence.
[^11]: **Pronoun** (ნაცვალსახელი, *natsvalsakheli*) — A word that takes the place of a noun.
[^12]: **Preverb** (ზმნისწინი, *zmnists'ini*) — A prefix attached to a verb that indicates direction, adds meaning, or changes the verb's aspect.
[^13]: **Suffix** (სუფიქსი / ბოლოსართი, *supiksi* / *bolosarti*) — A grammatical element added to the end of a word.
[^14]: **Aorist** (წყვეტილი მწკრივი, *ts'q'vet'ili mts'k'rivi*) — A specific past tense used for completed, simple actions without focusing on their duration.
[^15]: **Vowel** (ხმოვანი, *khmovani*) — A speech sound made with your mouth open, not blocked by your teeth, tongue, or lips.
[^16]: **Verb** (ზმნა, *zmna*) — An action word or word showing a state of being.
