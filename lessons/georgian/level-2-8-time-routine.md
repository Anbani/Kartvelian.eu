## 1. The Days of the Week (The Number Hack)

If you remember your numbers from Module 4, learning the Georgian days of the week will be incredibly easy. The system revolves around Saturday (**შაბათი** — *shabati*). Monday through Thursday are simply created by adding the roots of numbers 2, 3, 4, and 5 in front of the word for "Saturday."

*Literally, Monday is "Two-Saturday" and Thursday is "Five-Saturday."*

| Day | Georgian | Literal Translation / Origin | Transliteration |
|-----|----------|------------------------------|-----------------|
| Monday | **ორშაბათი** | ორ (*or-*, 2) + შაბათი (*shabati*) | *orshabati* |
| Tuesday | **სამშაბათი** | სამ (*sam-*, 3) + შაბათი (*shabati*) | *samshabati* |
| Wednesday | **ოთხშაბათი** | ოთხ (*otkh-*, 4) + შაბათი (*shabati*) | *otkhshabati* |
| Thursday | **ხუთშაბათი** | ხუთ (*khut-*, 5) + შაბათი (*shabati*) | *khutshabati* |
| Friday | **პარასკევი** | *(From Greek Paraskeuē, "Preparation")* | *p'arask'evi* |
| Saturday | **შაბათი** | *(Sabbath)* | *shabati* |
| Sunday | **კვირა** | *(From Greek Kyriakē; also means "week")* | *k'vira* |

### Saying "ON a Day"
To say you are doing something *on* a specific day, you don't use a postposition[^2] like "on" in English. Instead, you put the day into the **Dative case[^3]** by dropping the `-ი` (*-i*) and adding **-ს** (*-s*) (or simply adding **-ს** *-s* after `-ა` *-a*):

*   **ორშაბათს** (*orshabats*) — On Monday
*   **შაბათს** (*shabats*) — On Saturday
*   **კვირას** (*k'viras*) — On Sunday *(Ends in `-ა` *-a*, so keep the vowel and add `-ს` *-s*)*

## 2. The Months of the Year

The Georgian months are very similar to the English and Latin ones. Eleven of the twelve months end in the standard noun[^4] marker **-ი** (*-i*), while August (**აგვისტო**, *agvist'o*) ends in **-ო** (*-o*).

*   **იანვარი** (*ianvari* — Jan), **თებერვალი** (*tebervali* — Feb), **მარტი** (*mart'i* — Mar), **აპრილი** (*ap'rili* — Apr), **მაისი** (*maisi* — May), **ივნისი** (*ivnisi* — Jun), **ივლისი** (*ivlisi* — Jul), **აგვისტო** (*agvist'o* — Aug), **სექტემბერი** (*sekt'emberi* — Sep), **ოქტომბერი** (*okt'omberi* — Oct), **ნოემბერი** (*noemberi* — Nov), **დეკემბერი** (*dek'emberi* — Dec).

### Saying "IN a Month"
To say something happens *in* a month, use the postposition **-ში** (*-shi*, from Module 5). Drop the final `-ი` (*-i*) for months ending in `-ი`, and keep the `-ო` (*-o*) for August:

*   **აგვისტოში** (*agvist'oshi*) — In August
*   **მაისში** (*maisshi*) — In May

## 3. Telling Time: The Basics

Time in Georgian is conceptually different from English, especially when it comes to half-hours. 

### Question 1: What time is it?
**რომელი საათია?** (*romeli saatia?*) — Literally: "Which hour is it?"

To answer with an exact hour, say the number + **საათია** (*saatia* — "is the hour"). Note that **1:00** is the one exception, using **პირველი** (*p'irveli*, "first") instead of **ერთი** (*erti*, "one"):
*   1:00 — **პირველი საათია.** (*p'irveli saatia*)
*   5:00 — **ხუთი საათია.** (*khuti saatia*)
*   12:00 — **თორმეტი საათია.** (*tormet'i saatia*)

### The "Half-Past" Trap
In English, 2:30 is "half past two." In Georgian, it is **"the half of three."** You always look *forward* to the next hour. The formula is: **[Next Hour in Genitive Case[^1], `-ის` *-is*] + ნახევარია (*nakhevaria* — "is half")**.

*   12:30 — **პირველის ნახევარია.** (*p'irvelis nakhevaria* — *Half of first [1:00]*)
*   1:30 — **ორის ნახევარია.** (*oris nakhevaria* — *Half of two*)
*   4:30 — **ხუთის ნახევარია.** (*khutis nakhevaria* — *Half of five*)
*   8:30 — **ცხრის ნახევარია.** (*tskhris nakhevaria* — *Half of nine*)

### Question 2: AT what time?
**რომელ საათზე?** (*romel saatze?*) — At what time?
To say an event happens *at* a certain time, use the postposition **-ზე** (*-ze*, "on/at"). Numbers ending in `-ი` (*-i*) drop their final `-ი` before **საათზე** (*saatze*), while vowel-stem numbers like **რვა** (*rva*, 8) and **ცხრა** (*tskhra*, 9) stay unchanged. For "at X:30," attach **-ზე** (*-ze*) to **ნახევარი** (*nakhevari* → **ნახევარზე**, *nakhevarze*):

*   **პირველ საათზე** (*p'irvel saatze*) — At 1 o'clock.
*   **შვიდ საათზე** (*shvid saatze*) — At 7 o'clock.
*   **რვა საათზე** (*rva saatze*) — At 8 o'clock.
*   **შვიდის ნახევარზე** (*shvidis nakhevarze*) — At 6:30 *(lit. "on the half of seven")*.

## 4. Useful Time Words & Adverbs of Frequency

To talk about your daily routine, you need words that describe *when* and *how often* things happen.

| Time Words | English | Frequency Adverbs | English |
|------------|---------|-------------------|---------|
| **დღეს** (*dghes*) | Today | **ყოველდღე** (*q'oveldghe*) | Every day |
| **ხვალ** (*khval*) | Tomorrow | **ხშირად** (*khshirad*) | Often |
| **გუშინ** (*gushin*) | Yesterday | **იშვიათად** (*ishviatad*) | Rarely |
| **დილით** (*dilit*) | In the morning | **ზოგჯერ** (*zogjer*) | Sometimes |
| **საღამოს** (*saghamos*) | In the evening | **არასდროს** (*arasdros*) | Never |

## 5. Putting It Together: My Daily Routine

Read this short paragraph describing a typical routine. Pay attention to how the time words, days of the week, and the motion verbs and transport forms from Module 7 interact.

> **ყოველდღე დილით რვა საათზე ვსვამ ყავას. ორშაბათს და ოთხშაბათს ცხრა საათზე მივდივარ სამსახურში. მე ხშირად დავდივარ ფეხით. საღამოს შვიდის ნახევარზე მოვდივარ სახლში.**
>
> *Q'oveldghe dilit rva saatze vsvam q'avas. Orshabats da otkhshabats tskhra saatze mivdivar samsakhurshi. Me khshirad davdivar pekhit. Saghamos shvidis nakhevarze movdivar sakhlshi.*

*Translation Breakdown:*
*   *Every day in the morning at eight o'clock I drink (**ვსვამ**, vsvam) coffee (**ყავას**, q'avas).*
*   *On Monday and on Wednesday at nine o'clock I go (**მივდივარ**, mivdivar) to work (**სამსახურში**, samsakhurshi).*
*   *I often go regularly (**დავდივარ**, davdivar) on foot (**ფეხით**, pekhit).*
*   *In the evening at half-past six (half of seven) I come (**მოვდივარ**, movdivar) home (**სახლში**, sakhlshi).*

### Next Steps
- Write out what time you usually wake up using the `[Number] საათზე` (*saatze*) format.
- Look at a clock. Practice saying what time it is, especially at the half-hour (remember to look to the *next* hour for the half!).
- Say out loud what day of the week it is today: **დღეს არის [Day].** (*dghes aris [Day].*)

When you can confidently discuss your schedule, you have completed Level 2 and are ready for **Level 3, Module 9: Present Tense Overview!**

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Genitive case** (ნათესაობითი ბრუნვა, *natesaobiti brunva*) — A grammatical case showing possession or relationship, similar to English "of" or "'s".
[^2]: **Postposition** (თანდებული, *tandebuli*) — A word or bound suffix functioning like an English preposition (e.g., in, on, at), but attached to the end of a noun.
[^3]: **Dative case** (მიცემითი ბრუნვა, *mitsemiti brunva*) — A grammatical case used for indirect objects, time expressions ("on Monday"), or subjects with certain experiential verbs.
[^4]: **Noun** (არსებითი სახელი, *arsebiti sakheli*) — A word that represents a person, place, thing, or idea.
