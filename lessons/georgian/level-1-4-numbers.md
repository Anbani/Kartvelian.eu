Welcome to Module 4! If you've ever studied French and wrestled with *quatre-vingts* (four-twenties for 80), you have a head start here. 

Georgian numbers use a **vigesimal (base-20) system**. Instead of counting by tens (thirty, forty, fifty), Georgians count by twenties. The number 55 is literally spoken as "two-twenties-and-fifteen." 

It requires a little bit of mental math at first, but once you memorize 1 through 20, the rest of the system is entirely logical.

---

## 1. The Core: Numbers 0 to 10

You must memorize these perfectly. They are the building blocks for every other number in the language (and **0** is **ნული**, *nuli*).

| Number | Georgian | Transliteration | Number | Georgian | Transliteration |
|--------|----------|-----------------|--------|----------|-----------------|
| **1** | ერთი | *erti* | **6** | ექვსი | *ekvsi* |
| **2** | ორი | *ori* | **7** | შვიდი | *shvidi* |
| **3** | სამი | *sami* | **8** | რვა | *rva* |
| **4** | ოთხი | *otkhi* | **9** | ცხრა | *tskhra* |
| **5** | ხუთი | *khuti* | **10** | ათი | *ati* |

> **🗣️ Pronunciation Check:** Pay attention to **რვა** (*rva*, 8) and **ცხრა** (*tskhra*, 9). Remember the rule from Module 1: blend the consonant[^1] clusters tightly together without adding an "uh" sound!

## 2. The Teens (11–19)

To make a "teen" number, Georgian uses a sandwich approach. You take the root[^5] of the numbers 1–9, place **თ-** (*t-*, a reduced form of **ათი** *ati*, "ten") on the front, and add **-მეტი** (*-met'i*) to the end. (**მეტი**, *met'i*, means "more," so 11 is literally "ten-one-more").

*There are a few regular sound mergers for ease of pronunciation, but the pattern is highly consistent.*

| Math | Formula (`თ-` *t-* + root + `-მეტი` *-met'i*) | Georgian | Transliteration |
|------|-----------------------------------------------|----------|-----------------|
| 10+1 | თ- (*t-*) + ერ(თ) (*ert-*) + -მეტი (*-met'i*) | **თერთმეტი** | *tertmet'i* |
| 10+2 | თ- (*t-*) + ორ (*or-*) + -მეტი (*-met'i*) | **თორმეტი** | *tormet'i* |
| 10+3 | *თ- (t-) + სამ (sam-) + -მეტი (-met'i)* (merged) | **ცამეტი** | *tsamet'i* |
| 10+4 | თ- (*t-*) + ოთხ (*otkh-*) + -მეტი (*-met'i*) | **თოთხმეტი** | *totkhmet'i* |
| 10+5 | თ- (*t-*) + ხუთ (*khut-*) + -მეტი (*-met'i*) | **თხუთმეტი** | *tkhutmet'i* |
| 10+6 | თ- (*t-*) + ექვს (*ekvs-*) + -მეტი (*-met'i*) | **თექვსმეტი** | *tekvsmet'i* |
| 10+7 | *თ- (t-) + შვიდ (shvid-) + -მეტი (-met'i)* (merged) | **ჩვიდმეტი** | *chvidmet'i* |
| 10+8 | *თ- (t-) + რვა (rva-) + -მეტი (-met'i)* (`რვ` → `ვრ`) | **თვრამეტი** | *tvramet'i* |
| 10+9 | *თ- (t-) + ცხრა (tskhra-) + -მეტი (-met'i)* (merged) | **ცხრამეტი** | *tskhramet'i* |

## 3. The Pillars: Counting by 20s

Because Georgian is a base-20 system, your main "pillars" are multiples of 20. 
*   40 is "two-twenties" (**ორ**-მ-**ოცი**, *or-m-otsi*)
*   60 is "three-twenties" (**სამ**-**ოცი**, *sam-otsi*)
*   80 is "four-twenties" (**ოთხ**-მ-**ოცი**, *otkh-m-otsi*)

| Number | Math | Georgian | Transliteration |
|--------|------|----------|-----------------|
| **20** | 1 × 20 | **ოცი** | *otsi* |
| **40** | 2 × 20 | **ორმოცი** | *ormotsi* |
| **60** | 3 × 20 | **სამოცი** | *samotsi* |
| **80** | 4 × 20 | **ოთხმოცი** | *otkhmotsi* |
| **100** | 100 | **ასი** | *asi* |

## 4. The Vigesimal System (Doing the Math for 21–99)

To make any number between the pillars, you use this formula:
**[The 20-Pillar] + და (*da* — "and") + [Number from 1–19]**

When you connect them, the final `ი` (*-i*) at the end of the 20-pillar drops off, and the parts merge into one word. This includes **30** (20+10 = **ოცდაათი**, *otsdaati*), **50** (40+10 = **ორმოცდაათი**, *ormotsdaati*), **70** (60+10 = **სამოცდაათი**, *samotsdaati*), and **90** (80+10 = **ოთხმოცდაათი**, *otkhmotsdaati*). Let's practice the mental math:

**Example: 25**
1.  Math: 20 + 5
2.  Components: *ოცი* (*otsi*, 20) + *და* (*da*, and) + *ხუთი* (*khuti*, 5)
3.  Merged: **ოცდახუთი** (*otsdakhuti*)

**Example: 53**
1.  Math: 40 + 13 *(Because 53 falls between 40 and 60)*
2.  Components: *ორმოცი* (*ormotsi*, 40) + *და* (*da*, and) + *ცამეტი* (*tsamet'i*, 13)
3.  Merged: **ორმოცდაცამეტი** (*ormotsdatsamet'i*)

**Example: 71**
1.  Math: 60 + 11
2.  Components: *სამოცი* (*samotsi*, 60) + *და* (*da*, and) + *თერთმეტი* (*tertmet'i*, 11)
3.  Merged: **სამოცდათერთმეტი** (*samotsdatertmet'i*)

## 5. Currency & The "Singular[^2] Noun[^4]" Rule

The official currency of Georgia is the **Lari** (**ლარი**, *lari* — GEL). 
One Lari is divided into 100 **Tetri** (**თეთრი**, *tetri*). *Fun fact: **თეთრი** (*tetri*) literally means "white," referring to silver coins from antiquity.*

Before we talk about money, you must learn one of the most important rules in Georgian grammar:

> **🚨 THE GOLDEN RULE OF NUMBERS:** In Georgian, **any number greater than 1 must be followed by a SINGULAR noun.** You never pluralize a noun when a number or quantity word precedes it!

**Incorrect:** ორი ძაღლები (*ori dzaghlebi*, Two dogs — *uses plural[^3]*) ❌
**Correct:** ორი ძაღლი (*ori dzaghli*, Two dogs — *uses singular*) ✅

**Using Money:**
*   1 Lari = **ერთი ლარი** (*erti lari*)
*   5 Lari = **ხუთი ლარი** (*khuti lari*) *[Not larebi!]*
*   2 Lari and 50 Tetri = **ორი ლარი და ორმოცდაათი თეთრი** (*ori lari da ormotsdaati tetri*)

## 6. Shopping: "How much is it?"

Armed with your numbers, you can now navigate markets, bakeries, and cafes. 

### Essential Vocabulary
*   **რა ღირს?** (*ra ghirs?*) — How much does it cost?
*   **გაქვთ...?** (*gakvt...?*) — Do you have...? *(Formal)*
*   **მომეცით, გთხოვთ.** (*mometsit, gtkhovt*) — Give me, please. *(Formal)*
*   **ეს მინდა.** (*es minda*) — I want this.
*   **ხურდა** (*khurda*) — Change (coins / small bills).

### Practice Dialogue: The Bakery
**თქვენ** (*tkven*, You): გამარჯობა. პური გაქვთ? (*Gamarjoba. P'uri gakvt?* — *Hello. Do you have bread?*)
**გამყიდველი** (*gamq'idveli*, Seller): დიახ, გვაქვს. (*Diakh, gvakvs.* — *Yes, we have it.*)
**თქვენ** (*tkven*, You): რა ღირს? (*Ra ghirs?* — *How much is it?*)
**გამყიდველი** (*gamq'idveli*, Seller): ერთი ლარი და ოთხმოცი თეთრი. (*Erti lari da otkhmotsi tetri.* — *1.80 Lari.*)
**თქვენ** (*tkven*, You): ერთი პური მომეცით, გთხოვთ. (*Erti p'uri mometsit, gtkhovt.* — *Give me one bread, please.*)

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Consonant** (თანხმოვანი, *tankhmovani*) — A speech sound that is not a vowel.
[^2]: **Singular** (მხოლობითი რიცხვი, *mkholobiti ritskhvi*) — A grammatical form indicating one person or thing.
[^3]: **Plural** (მრავლობითი რიცხვი, *mravlobiti ritskhvi*) — A grammatical form indicating more than one person or thing.
[^4]: **Noun** (არსებითი სახელი, *arsebiti sakheli*) — A word that represents a person, place, thing, or idea.
[^5]: **Root** (ძირი, *dziri*) — The most basic, irreducible part of a word that carries its fundamental meaning.
