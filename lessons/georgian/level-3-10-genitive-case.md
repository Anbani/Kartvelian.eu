Welcome to Module 10! By now, you have learned how to talk about where things are (Postpositions), who is receiving an action (The Dative Case[^2]), and how present-tense verbs[^8] work. 

Now, it is time to talk about who *owns* things. In English, we show possession by adding an apostrophe-s ("John**'s** car") or by using the word "of" ("The capital **of** Georgia"). 

Georgian does both of these jobs with one incredibly elegant tool: **The Genitive Case[^1]**. In this module, we will learn how to form it, use it to describe family relationships, and use it to combine nouns.

---

## 1. Essential Family Vocabulary

Because the best way to practice possession is by talking about family, you first need to learn your relatives. 

> **🧠 Brain Teaser / Cultural Tip:** Georgian flips the script on the most basic human words. When a Georgian baby cries **მამა!** (*mama!*), they are calling for their father—while **დედა** (*deda*) means mother!

| Georgian | Transliteration | English |
|----------|-----------------|---------|
| დედა | *deda* | Mother |
| მამა | *mama* | Father |
| და | *da* | Sister |
| ძმა | *dzma* | Brother |
| ბებია | *bebia* | Grandmother |
| ბაბუა | *babua* | Grandfather |
| შვილი | *shvili* | Child / Son / Daughter |

## 2. Forming the Genitive Case (`-ის` / `-ს` — *-is* / *-s*)

The primary marker for the Genitive case is **-ის** (*-is*). However, depending on the vowel[^7] at the end of the noun[^9], the rules change slightly. 

### Rule A: Common nouns ending in `-ი` (*-i*), `-ა` (*-a*), or `-ე` (*-e*)
For most standard nouns ending in `-ი`, `-ა`, or `-ე`, you **drop** the final vowel and attach **-ის** (*-is*).

| Dictionary Form | Drop Vowel | Genitive Form | Transliteration | English Meaning |
|-----------------|------------|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| ძაღლ**ი** (*dzaghli*, Dog) | ძაღლ- (*dzaghl-*) | **ძაღლის** | *dzaghlis* | The dog's... |
| დედ**ა** (*deda*, Mother) | დედ- (*ded-*) | **დედის** | *dedis* | Mother's... |
| ხ**ე** (*khe*, Tree) | ხ- (*kh-*) | **ხის** | *khis* | The tree's... / Wooden |
| ვაშლ**ი** (*vashli*, Apple) | ვაშლ- (*vashl-*) | **ვაშლის** | *vashlis* | The apple's... |

### Rule B: Nouns ending in `-ო` (*-o*) or `-უ` (*-u*)
If a word ends in `-ო` (*-o*) or `-უ` (*-u*), you **keep** the vowel and simply attach **-ს** (*-s*).

| Dictionary Form | Rule | Genitive Form | Transliteration | English Meaning |
|-----------------|------|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| გოგ**ო** (*gogo*, Girl) | Keep `ო` (*-o*), add `-ს` (*-s*) | **გოგოს** | *gogos* | The girl's... |
| ბ**უ** (*bu*, Owl) | Keep `უ` (*-u*), add `-ს` (*-s*) | **ბუს** | *bus* | The owl's... |

### Rule C: Proper Nouns (Names of people and places)
Proper names ending in a vowel (**-ა**, **-ე**, **-ო**, **-უ**) or whose stem ends in **-ი** (like **გიორგი**, *Giorgi*) **keep their final vowel and attach `-ს` (*-s*)** (while consonant-stem names ending in nominative `-ი`, such as **დავითი** *Daviti* or **მარიამი** *Mariami*, drop `-ი` and take **-ის** *-is*: **დავითის** *Davitis*, **მარიამის** *Mariamis*).

| Name | Genitive Form | Transliteration | English Meaning |
|------|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| ან**ა** (*Ana*) | **ანას** | *Anas* | Ana's... |
| ელენ**ე** (*Elene*) | **ელენეს** | *Elenes* | Elene's... |
| გიორგ**ი** (*Giorgi*) | **გიორგის** | *Giorgis* | Giorgi's... |
| საქართველ**ო** (*Sakartvelo*, Georgia) | **საქართველოს** | *Sakartvelos* | Georgia's... / Of Georgia |

## 3. Possession: Putting It Together

The word order in Georgian is the same as with English `'s`: **[Owner in Genitive] + [Possessed Noun]**.

*   **ანას ძაღლი** (*Anas dzaghli*) — Ana's dog.
*   **გიორგის მანქანა** (*Giorgis mankana*) — Giorgi's car.
*   **დედის სახლი** (*dedis sakhli*) — Mother's house.
*   **ჩემი ძმის მეგობარი** (*chemi dzmis megobari*) — My brother's friend. *(Notice how **ძმა**, *dzma*, drops `-ა` *-a* to become **ძმის**, *dzmis*.)*

---

## 4. The Possessive Pronouns

You don't put the Genitive suffix on words like "I" or "You." Instead, they have dedicated possessive adjectives/pronouns, all ending in **-ი** (*-i*):

| Pronoun[^5] | Possessive (Georgian) | Transliteration | English |
|-------------|-----------------------|-----------------|---------|
| მე (*me*, I) | **ჩემი** | *chemi* | My |
| შენ (*shen*, You) | **შენი** | *sheni* | Your (informal) |
| ის (*is*, He/She) | **მისი** | *misi* | His / Her / Its |
| ჩვენ (*chven*, We) | **ჩვენი** | *chveni* | Our |
| თქვენ (*tkven*, You) | **თქვენი** | *tkveni* | Your (formal/plural[^6]) |
| ისინი (*isini*, They) | **მათი** | *mati* | Their |

**Examples:**
*   **ეს ჩემი და არის.** (*es chemi da aris*) — This is my sister.
*   **სად არის თქვენი შვილი?** (*sad aris tkveni shvili?*) — Where is your child?

> **🗣️ Kinship Shortcut:** With **დედა** (*deda*, mother) and **მამა** (*mama*, father), Georgians usually attach **ჩემი** (*chemi*, "my") directly to the end of the word: **დედაჩემი** (*dedachemi*, "my mother" → Genitive **დედაჩემის**, *dedachemis*, "my mother's") and **მამაჩემი** (*mamachemi*, "my father" → Genitive **მამაჩემის**, *mamachemis*, "my father's").

## 5. Nouns Modifying Nouns (The "Of" Connection)

In English, we often stack nouns together without changing them, like saying "apple juice" or "wine glass," where the first noun acts like an adjective[^3]. 

In Georgian, you cannot simply stack two dictionary nouns. The modifying noun must be put into the **Genitive case** in front of the head noun—literally "apple's juice" or "wine's glass":

| Noun 1 (Modifier) | Noun 2 (Object) | Combined (Genitive + Noun) | Transliteration | English |
|-------------------|-----------------|----------------------------|-----------------|---------|
| ვაშლი (*vashli*, Apple) | წვენი (*ts'veni*, Juice) | **ვაშლის წვენი** | *vashlis ts'veni* | Apple juice |
| ღვინო (*ghvino*, Wine) | ჭიქა (*ch'ika*, Glass) | **ღვინის ჭიქა** | *ghvinis ch'ika* | Wine glass |
| ხე (*khe*, Wood/Tree) | მაგიდა (*magida*, Table) | **ხის მაგიდა** | *khis magida* | Wooden table |
| საქართველო (*Sakartvelo*, Georgia) | ისტორია (*ist'oria*, History) | **საქართველოს ისტორია** | *Sakartvelos ist'oria* | History of Georgia |

> **⚠️ Note on `ღვინო` (*ghvino*):** **ღვინო** (*ghvino*, "wine") is a classic lexical exception to Rule B—it behaves like a consonant[^4]/truncating stem[^10] in oblique cases, dropping `-ო` (*-o*) and taking **-ის** (*-is*) rather than **-ს** (*-s*). Hence **ღვინის ჭიქა** (*ghvinis ch'ika*), not `ღვინოს ჭიქა` (*ghvinos ch'ika*). A handful of older native `-ო` nouns (such as **დრო** *dro* → **დროის** *drois*, "of time") take **-ის** (*-is*), whereas loanwords and most `-ო` nouns follow Rule B (**გოგო** *gogo* → **გოგოს** *gogos*, **საქართველო** *Sakartvelo* → **საქართველოს** *Sakartvelos*).

## 6. Practice Dialogue: Family and Belongings

Read this conversation out loud. Pay attention to how the Genitive case is used to show relationships and ownership.

**მარიამი** (*Mariami*, Mariam): გამარჯობა, დავით. ვისი მანქანაა ეს? (*Gamarjoba, Davit. Visi mankanaa es?* — *Hello, Davit. Whose car is this?*)
**დავითი** (*Daviti*, Davit): ეს ჩემი ძმის მანქანაა. (*Es chemi dzmis mankanaa.* — *This is my brother's car.*)
**მარიამი** (*Mariami*, Mariam): ძალიან ლამაზია. შენი ძმა სად არის? (*Dzalian lamazia. Sheni dzma sad aris?* — *It is very beautiful. Where is your brother?*)
**დავითი** (*Daviti*, Davit): ჩემი ძმა ახლა საქართველოს ბანკშია. (*Chemi dzma akhla Sakartvelos bank'shia.* — *My brother is in the Bank of Georgia now.*)
**მარიამი** (*Mariami*, Mariam): გასაგებია. ეს ანას ჩანთაა? (*Gasagebia. Es Anas chantaa?* — *Understood. Is this Ana's bag?*)
**დავითი** (*Daviti*, Davit): კი, ეგ მისი ჩანთაა. (*K'i, eg misi chantaa.* — *Yes, that is her bag.*)

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Genitive case** (ნათესაობითი ბრუნვა, *natesaobiti brunva*) — A grammatical case showing possession or relationship, similar to English "of" or "'s".
[^2]: **Dative case** (მიცემითი ბრუნვა, *mitsemiti brunva*) — A grammatical case used for indirect objects, or subjects with certain experiential verbs.
[^3]: **Adjective** (ზედსართავი სახელი, *zedsartavi sakheli*) — A word that describes or modifies a noun.
[^4]: **Consonant** (თანხმოვანი, *tankhmovani*) — A speech sound that is not a vowel.
[^5]: **Pronoun** (ნაცვალსახელი, *natsvalsakheli*) — A word that takes the place of a noun.
[^6]: **Plural** (მრავლობითი რიცხვი, *mravlobiti ritskhvi*) — A grammatical form indicating more than one person or thing.
[^7]: **Vowel** (ხმოვანი, *khmovani*) — A speech sound made with your mouth open, not blocked by your teeth, tongue, or lips.
[^8]: **Verb** (ზმნა, *zmna*) — An action word or word showing a state of being.
[^9]: **Noun** (არსებითი სახელი, *arsebiti sakheli*) — A word that represents a person, place, thing, or idea.
[^10]: **Stem** (ფუძე, *pudze*) — The core, basic part of a word before endings are attached.
