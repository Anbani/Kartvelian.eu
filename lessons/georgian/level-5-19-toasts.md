Welcome to Module 19! While we briefly introduced the *Supra* (feast) and the role of the *Tamada* (toastmaster) in the previous lesson, we are now going to master the **სადღეგრძელო** (*sadghegrdzelo* — toast).

Knowing how to deliver and respond to a toast is one of the most respected skills you can show as a guest in Georgia. A full supra is not five toasts but a long, structured evening of fifteen to twenty — a communal history lesson and philosophical dialogue, not an excuse to drink.

---

## 1. The Canonical Sequence

A traditional supra follows a strict architecture. The Tamada can embellish a theme or pass the floor (**ალავერდი**, *alaverdi*), but he **cannot reorder the foundation**: the first five to eight toasts are virtually fixed. The classic progression is *God → the gathering → the host → the dead → the living*.

The response to almost every toast is **გაუმარჯოს!** (*gaumarjos!* — literally "may it be victorious," i.e. "cheers to it!").

> **The heart of the philosophy:** the toast to the dead comes in the *first half* of the meal, never at the end — and it is immediately followed by the toast to *life and children*. That leap from silence and no-clinking straight back to joy is the whole point: the circle of life continues.

## 2. Adapting to the Occasion

The skeleton above is the everyday supra. Specific occasions reshape it — adding, reordering, or (in one case) forbidding parts of the ritual.

### დაბადების დღე (*dabadebis dghe* — birthday)
The whole table centers on the **იუბილარი** (*iubilari* — the person celebrating). The "reason for the gathering" toast becomes a long eulogy of their life and character, and toasts to their parents, spouse, and closest friends move up the order.
*   **გილოცავ დაბადების დღეს** (*gilotsav dabadebis dghes*) — Happy birthday (lit. "I congratulate you on your birthday").
*   **ბევრი ტკბილი წელი** (*bevri t'k'bili ts'eli*) — Many sweet years.

### ქელეხი (*kelekhi* — funeral / wake feast)
The most rigid supra of all: somber, strictly ordered, led by a specialist **მექელეხე თამადა** (*mekelekhe tamada*). **Clinking glasses is absolutely forbidden** (ჭიქის მიჭახუნება იკრძალება, *ch'ikis mich'akhuneba ik'rdzaleba*). The number of toasts is capped — usually 7, 9, or 13 — honoring the deceased and comforting the family. It **always** ends on a toast to life and children, so the meal closes on continuation, not grief.
*   **მიცვალებულის ხსოვნა** (*mitsvalebulis khsovna*) — the memory of the departed.
*   **ღმერთმა აცხონოს** (*ghmertma atskhonos*) — may God absolve them.

### ქორწილი (*korts'ili* — wedding)
Joyous and symbolic, focused on the merging of two lineages. Mandatory new toasts are inserted early in the canon for the couple and the wedding party.
*   **ნეფე-პატარძალი** (*nepe-p'at'ardzali*) — groom and bride.
*   **მეჯვარეები** (*mejvareebi*) — the best men / maids of honor (witnesses).
*   **მაყრიონი** (*maq'rioni*) — the wedding procession / party.
*   **ტკბილად დააბეროს ღმერთმა** (*t'k'bilad daaberos ghmertma*) — may God let them grow old together sweetly.

### ნათლობა (*natloba* — baptism)
Centers on spiritual kinship — in Georgia, godparents become lifelong family. Extra toasts honor the bond between the two families and the godparents' responsibility.
*   **ნათლული** (*natluli*) — godchild. **ნათლიები** (*natliebi*) — godparents.
*   **ღმერთმა ქრისტიანულად გაზარდოს** (*ghmertma krist'ianulad gazardos*) — may God raise the child a Christian.

## 3. Key Vocabulary for Toasting

*   **ჭიქა (Ch'ika):** Glass.
*   **ყანწი (Q'ants'i):** The drinking horn — a special vessel; a horn, once raised, is generally drained to the end.
*   **ბოლომდე (Bolomde):** "To the end." If the Tamada says "ბოლომდე!" (*bolomde!*), empty your glass completely. This is only mandatory for a few solemn toasts (the departed, the reason for gathering) or when drinking from the *q'ants'i* — for most toasts, sipping is fine.
*   **ალავერდი (Alaverdi):** The passing of the toast — when the Tamada hands the floor to someone else to elaborate on the current theme.
*   **ვახტანგური (Vakht'anguri):** A toast where two people link arms and drink their glasses empty, cementing their friendship.

---

## 4. The Rules of Engagement

1.  **Never interrupt the Tamada:** when he speaks, the table is quiet.
2.  **No sipping without a toast:** you don't drink casually — you wait for a dedication to be made and finished before you drink.
3.  **The "Alaverdi" response:** if the *alaverdi* is passed to you, stand up, continue the Tamada's theme, add your own heartfelt thoughts, then drink.
4.  **Clinking glasses:** younger or junior people aim their glass slightly lower than the other person's as a sign of respect — and never clink at all during the toast to the dead.

## 5. Crafting Your Own Toast

If you are a guest, you will eventually be asked to make a toast. Keep it heartfelt and simple.

> **Example of a Guest's Toast:**
>
> "დიდი მადლობა მასპინძლობისთვის. ძალიან მიყვარს საქართველო და ქართველები. თქვენს ოჯახს და ჩვენს მეგობრობას გაუმარჯოს!"
> *(Didi madloba masp'indzlobistvis. Dzalian miq'vars Sakartvelo da kartvelebi. Tkvens ojakhs da chvens megobrobas gaumarjos!)*
>
> "Thank you very much for your hospitality. I love Georgia and Georgians very much. Cheers to your family and our friendship!"

Raise your glass, look everyone in the eye, and say it with confidence!
