Welcome to Module 7! If you want to get out and explore Georgia, you need to know how to move. 

Georgian verbs of motion are incredibly logical. They function like Lego blocks: you take a core "movement" stem[^11] and snap on a directional prefix[^6] to indicate where you are going. In this module, we will conquer the core verbs "to go" and "to come," learn how to hail a taxi using the Instrumental Case[^1], and master basic street directions.

---

## 1. The Lego Blocks: Directional Prefixes

In English, we use different base words for different directions (e.g., *go* vs. *come*). In Georgian, the core verb[^9] remains the same; only the prefix at the very beginning changes.

*   **მი-** (*mi-*) means movement **away** from the speaker or current point.
*   **მო-** (*mo-*) means movement **towards** the speaker or listener.
*   **და-** (*da-*) means **habitual or multi-directional** movement (e.g., **დავდივარ**, *davdivar* — "I go regularly / I walk around").

The core 1st-person singular stem for moving in the present tense[^2] is **-ვდივარ** (*-vdivar*, where **ვ-** *v-* marks "I" and **-დი-** *-di-* is the motion stem). 

If we snap them together:
*   მი- (*mi-*) + ვ- (*v-*) + -დივარ (*-divar*) = **მივდივარ** (*mivdivar*) — I am going (away from here).
*   მო- (*mo-*) + ვ- (*v-*) + -დივარ (*-divar*) = **მოვდივარ** (*movdivar*) — I am coming (towards here/you).

> **🗣️ Pro Tip:** Imagine you are talking on the phone with a friend who is waiting for you at a cafe. You would say **მოვდივარ** (*movdivar* — "I am coming *to you*"). If you are leaving your house to go to the store, you say **მაღაზიაში მივდივარ** (*maghaziashi mivdivar* — "I am going *away from here* to the store").

## 2. Conjugating "To Go" (**მივდივარ**, *mivdivar*)

Notice how the `ვ` (*v*) is a 1st-person marker, meaning it only appears when "I" or "We" are the ones moving.

| Pronoun[^5] | Verb | Transliteration | English |
|-------------|------|-----------------|---------|
| მე (*me*, I) | **მივდივარ** | *mivdivar* | I am going |
| შენ (*shen*, You) | **მიდიხარ** | *midikhar* | You are going |
| ის (*is*, He/She) | **მიდის** | *midis* | He/She is going |
| ჩვენ (*chven*, We) | **მივდივართ** | *mivdivart* | We are going |
| თქვენ (*tkven*, You pl./formal) | **მიდიხართ** | *midikhart* | You (all/formal) are going |
| ისინი (*isini*, They) | **მიდიან** | *midian* | They are going |

---

## 3. Conjugating "To Come" (**მოვდივარ**, *movdivar*)

To change "going" into "coming," simply swap the **მი-** (*mi-*) prefix for **მო-** (*mo-*). The rest of the conjugation[^3] remains identical!

| Pronoun | Verb | Transliteration | English |
|---------|------|-----------------|---------|
| მე (*me*, I) | **მოვდივარ** | *movdivar* | I am coming |
| შენ (*shen*, You) | **მოდიხარ** | *modikhar* | You are coming |
| ის (*is*, He/She) | **მოდის** | *modis* | He/She is coming |
| ჩვენ (*chven*, We) | **მოვდივართ** | *movdivart* | We are coming |
| თქვენ (*tkven*, You pl./formal) | **მოდიხართ** | *modikhart* | You (all/formal) are coming |
| ისინი (*isini*, They) | **მოდიან** | *modian* | They are coming |

## 4. The Instrumental Case: Modes of Transport

If someone asks **როგორ მიდიხარ?** (*rogor midikhar?* — "How are you going?"), you need to explain your mode of transportation. In English, we say "by car" or "by bus." In Georgian, we use the **Instrumental Case**, which is marked by the suffix[^7] **-ით** (*-it*) (or **-თი**, *-ti*, after non-truncating **-ო** *-o* / **-უ** *-u*). 

### The Transport Rules:
*   **Drop the vowel[^8]:** If the transport noun[^10] ends in **-ი** (*-i*) or **-ა** (*-a*), drop the final vowel and add **-ით** (*-it*).
*   **Keep the vowel:** If a transport noun ends in **-ო** or **-უ** (*-o*, *-u*), keep the vowel and add **-თი** (*-ti*): მეტრო (*met'ro*) → **მეტროთი** (*met'roti*). Nouns in **-ე** (*-e*) behave like **-ა** (*-a*) and drop it: ხე (*khe*, tree) → **ხით** (*khit*) (as do native **-ო** nouns like ღვინო *ghvino* → **ღვინით** *ghvinit*).

| Dictionary Noun | English | Instrumental Form | Transliteration | English Meaning |
|-----------------|---------|-------------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| ფეხ**ი** (*pekhi*) | Foot | **ფეხით** | *pekhit* | On foot / walking |
| ავტობუს**ი** (*avt'obusi*) | Bus | **ავტობუსით** | *avt'obusit* | By bus |
| ტაქს**ი** (*t'aksi*) | Taxi | **ტაქსით** | *t'aksit* | By taxi |
| მანქან**ა** (*mankana*) | Car | **მანქანით** | *mankanit* | By car |
| მეტრ**ო** (*met'ro*) | Metro | **მეტროთი** | *met'roti* | By metro |

**Examples:**
*   **მე სახლში ფეხით მივდივარ.** (*me sakhlshi pekhit mivdivar*) — I am going home on foot.
*   **ის მოდის ავტობუსით.** (*is modis avt'obusit*) — He/She is coming by bus.

## 5. Navigating the Streets: Directions

Whether you are in a taxi or asking for directions on the street, these five expressions are essential for survival:

*   **სად მიდიხართ?** (*sad midikhart?*) — Where are you going? *(Formal)*
*   **მარჯვნივ** (*marjvniv*) — To the right / Right.
*   **მარცხნივ** (*martskhniv*) — To the left / Left.
*   **პირდაპირ** (*p'irdap'ir*) — Straight ahead.
*   **აქ გააჩერეთ!** (*ak gaacheret!*) — Stop here! *(Formal, perfect for taxis!)*

> **⚠️ Pronunciation Challenge:** **მარჯვნივ** (*marjvniv*) and **მარცხნივ** (*martskhniv*) are notorious tongue twisters for beginners. Take them slowly. Remember the rule for consonant[^4] clusters: string them together smoothly without adding extra vowels (`marjv-niv` and `marts-khniv`).

## 6. Putting It All Together: A Taxi Ride Dialogue

Read this dialogue aloud. Notice how the driver asks where the passenger is going, and the passenger uses directional words to guide the driver.

**მძღოლი** (*mdzgholi*, Driver): გამარჯობა. სად მიდიხართ? (*Gamarjoba. Sad midikhart?* — *Hello. Where are you going?*)
**მგზავრი** (*mgzavri*, Passenger): გამარჯობა. ცენტრში მივდივარ. (*Gamarjoba. Tsent'rshi mivdivar.* — *Hello. I am going to the center.*)
**მძღოლი** (*mdzgholi*, Driver): მარჯვნივ თუ მარცხნივ? (*Marjvniv tu martskhniv?* — *Right or left?*)
**მგზავრი** (*mgzavri*, Passenger): ახლა პირდაპირ და მერე მარჯვნივ. (*Akhla p'irdap'ir da mere marjvniv.* — *Now straight ahead and then right.*)
*[A few minutes later...]*
**მგზავრი** (*mgzavri*, Passenger): აქ გააჩერეთ, გთხოვთ. (*Ak gaacheret, gtkhovt.* — *Stop here, please.*)

### Next Steps
- Memorize the prefixes **მი-** (*mi-*, away) and **მო-** (*mo-*, towards).
- Stand up, walk away from your desk, and say out loud: **მივდივარ!** (*mivdivar!*)
- Master the transport suffix **-ით** (*-it*) by practicing: **მე მივდივარ [Transport]-ით.** (*me mivdivar [Transport]-it.*)

Once you can direct a taxi smoothly, you are ready to schedule your day in **Module 8: Time, Days & Routine!**

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Instrumental case** (მოქმედებითი ბრუნვა, *mokmedebiti brunva*) — A grammatical case indicating the means, instrument, or mode of transport by which an action is performed.
[^2]: **Present tense** (ახლანდელი დრო, *akhlandeli dro*) — The tense used for actions happening right now or habitually.
[^3]: **Conjugation** (უღვლილება, *ughvlileba*) — The process of changing a verb's form to indicate tense, person, or number.
[^4]: **Consonant** (თანხმოვანი, *tankhmovani*) — A speech sound that is not a vowel.
[^5]: **Pronoun** (ნაცვალსახელი, *natsvalsakheli*) — A word that takes the place of a noun.
[^6]: **Prefix** (პრეფიქსი / თავსართი, *p'repiksi* / *tavsarti*) — A grammatical element added to the beginning of a word.
[^7]: **Suffix** (სუფიქსი / ბოლოსართი, *supiksi* / *bolosarti*) — A grammatical element added to the end of a word.
[^8]: **Vowel** (ხმოვანი, *khmovani*) — A speech sound made with your mouth open, not blocked by your teeth, tongue, or lips.
[^9]: **Verb** (ზმნა, *zmna*) — An action word or word showing a state of being.
[^10]: **Noun** (არსებითი სახელი, *arsebiti sakheli*) — A word that represents a person, place, thing, or idea.
[^11]: **Stem** (ფუძე, *pudze*) — The core, basic part of a word before endings are attached.
