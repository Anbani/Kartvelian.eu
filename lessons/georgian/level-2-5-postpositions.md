Welcome to Module 5! In English, when we want to describe where something is, we use *pre*-positions—little words that go *before* the noun[^5] (like **in** the house, **on** the table, **from** America).

Georgian does the exact opposite. It uses **postpositions**. These are small suffixes that attach directly to the *end* of the noun, like superglue. In this lesson, we will cover the "Big Four" location markers. Mastering these will instantly upgrade your ability to navigate the country.

---

## The Golden Rule for `-ში` (*-shi*) and `-ზე` (*-ze*)

Before we dive into the specific meanings, you need to know how to attach the postpositions to a noun. For **-ში** (*-shi*) and **-ზე** (*-ze*), the rule is brilliantly simple:

1.  **If the noun ends in `-ი` (*-i*):** Drop the `-ი` (*-i*) and attach the postposition[^1].
2.  **If the noun ends in `-ა`, `-ე`, `-ო`, or `-უ` (*-a*, *-e*, *-o*, *-u*):** Keep the vowel[^4] and attach the postposition right after it.

**-თან** (*-tan*) plays by a slightly different rule (see Section 3), and **-დან** (*-dan*) has its own pattern (Section 4).

---

## 1. `-ში` (*-shi*) : In / Inside / To (an enclosed place)

Use **-ში** (*-shi*) when you are physically inside—or going into—a building, a city, or a country. 

| Dictionary Form | Rule Application | Postposition Form | Transliteration | English |
|-----------------|------------------|-------------------|-----------------|---------|
| ბანკ**ი** (*bank'i*, Bank) | Drop the `ი` (*-i*) | **ბანკში** | *bank'shi* | In / to the bank |
| თბილის**ი** (*tbilisi*, Tbilisi) | Drop the `ი` (*-i*) | **თბილისში** | *tbilisshi* | In / to Tbilisi |
| სკოლ**ა** (*sk'ola*, School) | Keep the `ა` (*-a*) | **სკოლაში** | *sk'olashi* | In / to the school |
| ამერიკ**ა** (*amerik'a*, America) | Keep the `ა` (*-a*) | **ამერიკაში** | *amerik'ashi* | In / to America |
| კაფ**ე** (*k'ape*, Cafe) | Keep the `ე` (*-e*) | **კაფეში** | *k'apeshi* | In / to the cafe |

**Examples in action:**
*   **მე ვარ თბილისში.** (*me var tbilisshi*) — I am in Tbilisi.
*   **ის არის ბანკში.** (*is aris bank'shi*) — He/She is in the bank.

---

## 2. `-ზე` (*-ze*) : On / On top of / At (events)

Use **-ზე** (*-ze*) when something is resting on a surface. (It is also used for streets, avenues, and events.)

| Dictionary Form | Rule Application | Postposition Form | Transliteration | English |
|-----------------|------------------|-------------------|-----------------|---------|
| მაგიდ**ა** (*magida*, Table) | Keep the `ა` (*-a*) | **მაგიდაზე** | *magidaze* | On the table |
| სკამ**ი** (*sk'ami*, Chair) | Drop the `ი` (*-i*) | **სკამზე** | *sk'amze* | On the chair |
| რუსთაველ**ი** (*rustaveli*, Rustaveli) | Drop the `ი` (*-i*) | **რუსთაველზე** | *rustavelze* | On Rustaveli (Avenue) |
| ტელეფონ**ი** (*t'eleponi*, Phone) | Drop the `ი` (*-i*) | **ტელეფონზე** | *t'eleponze* | On the phone |

> **🗣️ Cultural Tip:** In English, you live "on" a street. In Georgian, it's the exact same logic. If someone asks where your hotel is, you can say: **რუსთაველზე** (*rustavelze* — "On Rustaveli Avenue"). 

---

## 3. `-თან` (*-tan*) : At / Near / With (a person)

This postposition is a multi-tool. It means "near" or "by" a physical location, and it is also the mandatory postposition when you are going **to a person** or hanging out **with a person**.

> **⚠️ The `-თან` (*-tan*) Twist:** Unlike **-ში** (*-shi*) and **-ზე** (*-ze*), the postposition **-თან** (*-tan*) requires a connecting **-ს-** (*-s-*) after *any* vowel-final noun (`-ა, -ე, -ო, -უ` — *-a, -e, -o, -u*), because it attaches to the dative case[^2] form (which ends in **-ს**, *-s*). Consonant[^3]-stem[^6] nouns (the `-ი` *-i* group) simply drop the `-ი` (*-i*) and add **-თან** (*-tan*).

| Dictionary Form | Rule Application | Postposition Form | Transliteration | English |
|-----------------|------------------|-------------------|-----------------|---------|
| სახლ**ი** (*sakhli*, House) | Drop `ი` (*-i*), add `-თან` (*-tan*) | **სახლთან** | *sakhltan* | Near the house |
| ექიმ**ი** (*ekimi*, Doctor) | Drop `ი` (*-i*), add `-თან` (*-tan*) | **ექიმთან** | *ekimtan* | At the doctor's |
| მეგობარ**ი** (*megobari*, Friend) | Drop `ი` (*-i*), add `-თან` (*-tan*) | **მეგობართან** | *megobartan* | With / at the friend's |
| დედ**ა** (*deda*, Mother) | Keep `ა` (*-a*), add `-სთან` (*-stan*) | **დედასთან** | *dedastan* | With / near mother |
| გოგ**ო** (*gogo*, Girl) | Keep `ო` (*-o*), add `-სთან` (*-stan*) | **გოგოსთან** | *gogostan* | With / near the girl |
| მეტრ**ო** (*met'ro*, Metro) | Keep `ო` (*-o*), add `-სთან` (*-stan*) | **მეტროსთან** | *met'rostan* | Near the metro |

**Examples in action:**
*   **გიორგი არის ექიმთან.** (*Giorgi aris ekimtan*) — Giorgi is at the doctor's.
*   **მე ვარ მეგობართან.** (*me var megobartan*) — I am with a friend (or at a friend's place).

---

## 4. `-დან` (*-dan*) : From (The Exception Rule)

The postposition **-დან** (*-dan*, "from") operates under slightly different rules because it historically merged with the Instrumental case ending **-ით** (*-it*). Pay close attention to words ending in **-ა** (*-a*)!

### The `-დან` (*-dan*) Rules:
1.  **If the noun ends in `-ი` (*-i*):** KEEP the `-ი` (*-i*) and add **-დან** (*-dan*) (producing **-იდან**, *-idan*).
2.  **If the noun ends in `-ა` (*-a*):** Drop the `-ა` (*-a*) and add **-იდან** (*-idan*).
3.  **If the noun ends in `-ე, -ო, -უ` (*-e, -o, -u*):** Keep the vowel and add **-დან** (*-dan*).

| Dictionary Form | Rule Application | Postposition Form | Transliteration | English |
|-----------------|------------------|-------------------|-----------------|---------|
| ბანკ**ი** (*bank'i*, Bank) | Keep the `ი` (*-i*) | **ბანკიდან** | *bank'idan* | From the bank |
| თბილის**ი** (*tbilisi*, Tbilisi) | Keep the `ი` (*-i*) | **თბილისიდან** | *tbilisidan* | From Tbilisi |
| ამერიკ**ა** (*amerik'a*, America) | **Change `ა` (*-a*) to `ი` (*-i*)** | **ამერიკიდან** | *amerik'idan* | From America |
| სკოლ**ა** (*sk'ola*, School) | **Change `ა` (*-a*) to `ი` (*-i*)** | **სკოლიდან** | *sk'olidan* | From school |
| მეტრ**ო** (*met'ro*, Metro) | Keep the `ო` (*-o*) | **მეტროდან** | *met'rodan* | From the metro |

## Putting It All Together: Motion + Postpositions

Let's combine these postpositions with the verbs of motion (**მივდივარ**, *mivdivar* / **მოვდივარ**, *movdivar*) that you will learn in Module 7. Look at how elegantly Georgian handles complex directions in just a few words:

**A Journey in Three Sentences:**
1.  **მე მოვდივარ ამერიკიდან.** (*me movdivar amerik'idan*) 
    *   *I am coming from America.*
2.  **ახლა ვარ თბილისში, რუსთაველზე.** (*akhla var tbilisshi, rustavelze*) 
    *   *Now I am in Tbilisi, on Rustaveli (Avenue).*
3.  **მივდივარ კაფეში, მეგობართან.** (*mivdivar k'apeshi, megobartan*) 
    *   *I am going to the cafe, to (meet) a friend.*

### Next Steps
- Take 3 places in your city (a park, a street, a bank) and practice adding **-ში** (*-shi*) and **-ზე** (*-ze*) to them.
- Look at your home country. Does it end in **-ა** (*-a*)? Practice saying "I am from [Country]" using the special **-დან** (*-dan*) rule (changing `-ა` *-a* to `-იდან` *-idan*).
- Say out loud: **მე ვარ საქართველოში!** (*me var sakartveloshi!* — "I am in Georgia!")

When you feel confident sticking these endings onto nouns, you are ready for **Module 6: The Dative Case & To Want / To Have!**

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Postposition** (თანდებული, *tandebuli*) — A word or bound suffix functioning like an English preposition (e.g., in, on, at), but attached to the end of a noun.
[^2]: **Dative case** (მიცემითი ბრუნვა, *mitsemiti brunva*) — A grammatical case used for indirect objects, or subjects with certain experiential verbs.
[^3]: **Consonant** (თანხმოვანი, *tankhmovani*) — A speech sound that is not a vowel.
[^4]: **Vowel** (ხმოვანი, *khmovani*) — A speech sound made with your mouth open, not blocked by your teeth, tongue, or lips.
[^5]: **Noun** (არსებითი სახელი, *arsebiti sakheli*) — A word that represents a person, place, thing, or idea.
[^6]: **Stem** (ფუძე, *pudze*) — The core, basic part of a word before endings are attached.
