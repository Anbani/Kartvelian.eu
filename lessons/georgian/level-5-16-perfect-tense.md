Welcome to Module 16! You have mastered the Present Tense[^2] (what is happening), the Future Tense[^5] (what will happen), the mighty Aorist[^14] (what *did* happen), and the Optative[^10] Mood (Module 15 — what *should* or *might* happen). 

But Georgian has a built-in lie detector. If you tell a story using the Aorist past tense[^9], you are grammatically implying: *"I know this happened because I saw it, or I was directly involved."* 

What if you didn't see it? What if you are passing on a rumor, reporting the news, or discovering a result after the fact? For this, Georgian uses the **Perfect Tense[^3]** (Evidentiality). 

---

## 1. The Magic Word: `თურმე` (turme)

Before we look at the verbs, you must learn the word that acts as the beacon for the Perfect tense: **თურმე (turme)**. 

It translates roughly to **"Apparently," "It turns out,"** or **"I just found out that..."** Georgians use this word constantly when gossiping, sharing news, or realizing something they hadn't noticed before.

*   **თურმე** ნიკა საქართველოშია! (turme Nik'a Sakartveloshia!) — *It turns out Nika is in Georgia!*
*   წვიმს **თურმე**! (ts'vims turme!) — *Oh, apparently it's raining! (I just looked out the window and realized).*

---

## 2. The Final Case Flip: The Dative Subject[^11]

Do you remember how transitive[^8] verbs forced the subject into the Ergative (`-მა` *-ma* / `-მ` *-m*) case in the past tense (Module 13) and the Optative mood (Module 15)? 

The Perfect tense introduces the final great "Case Flip." When using a transitive verb[^15] in the Perfect tense, the subject goes into the **Dative Case[^7]** (`-ს` *-s*), and the object stays in the **Nominative Case[^1]** (`-ი` *-i*). 

This happens because the Perfect tense behaves like an "Inversion Verb" (like *to want* or *to have* from Module 6). The grammar literally frames the sentence as: *"To him, the letter has apparently been written."*

Let's look at the complete evolution of a Georgian sentence across time:

| Tense / Mood | Subject | Verb | Object | Literal Grammar Translation |
|--------------|---------|------|--------|-----------------------------|
| **Present** | ნიკა (*Nik'a* - *Nom*) | წერს (*ts'ers*) | წერილ**ს** (*ts'erils* - *Dat*) | Nika writes the letter. |
| **Past (Aorist)**| ნიკა**მ** (*Nik'am* - *Erg*) | დაწერა (*dats'era*) | წერილ**ი** (*ts'erili* - *Nom*) | Nika wrote the letter (I saw it). |
| **Perfect** | ნიკა**ს** (*Nik'as* - *Dat*) | დაუწერია (*dauts'eria*) | წერილ**ი** (*ts'erili* - *Nom*) | To Nika, the letter has been written. |

## 3. Conjugating the Perfect (Reporting the News)

Because the Perfect tense is primarily used to report on what *other* people did when you weren't looking, the most important form to learn is the **3rd Person** (He/She/They).

### Transitive Verbs (Action)
To form the 3rd person Perfect of an action verb, you use the Preverb[^12], add an **-უ- (-u-)** after it, and end the verb with **-ია (-ia)** or **-ებია (-ebia)**. 

| Present | Aorist (Saw it) | Perfect (Heard about it) | Transliteration | English |
|---------|-----------------|--------------------------|-----------------|---------|
| აკეთებს (*ak'etebs*) | გააკეთა (*gaak'eta*) | **გაუკეთებია** | gauk'etebia | He/She apparently did it |
| წერს (*ts'ers*) | დაწერა (*dats'era*) | **დაუწერია** | dauts'eria | He/She apparently wrote it |
| ჭამს (*ch'ams*) | შეჭამა (*shech'ama*) | **შეუჭამია** | sheuch'amia | He/She apparently ate it |
| ყიდულობს (*q'idulobs*) | იყიდა (*iq'ida*) | **უყიდია** | uq'idia | He/She apparently bought it |

**Example:**
*   თურმე მარიამ**ს** მანქანა **უყიდია**! (turme Mariam**s** mankana **uq'idia**!) 
    *   *Apparently Mariam bought a car! (I didn't see her buy it, someone just told me).*

### Intransitive[^4] Verbs (State & Motion)
Just like in the past tense, intransitive verbs (like *to go* and *to be*) ignore the case flips. The subject stays strictly in the Nominative case (`-ი` *-i*). 

| Present | Aorist (Saw it) | Perfect (Heard about it) | Transliteration | English |
|---------|-----------------|--------------------------|-----------------|---------|
| არის (*aris*) | იყო (*iq'o*) | **ყოფილა** | q'opila | He/She has apparently been |
| მიდის (*midis*) | წავიდა (*ts'avida*) | **წასულა** | ts'asula | He/She has apparently gone |

**Example:**
*   თურმე გიორგი ამერიკაში **წასულა**. (turme Giorgi Amerik'ashi **ts'asula**.)
    *   *Apparently Giorgi went to America.*

## 4. The 1st Person Perfect: "I have never..."

While you usually report on *other* people, there is one massive exception where you will use the Perfect tense for yourself: **Talking about your life experiences, especially negative ones.**

If someone asks if you have ever been to a country or eaten a specific food, you use the 1st Person Perfect. 

*   **ყოფნა (*q'opna* - To be):** I have been -> `ვყოფილვარ` (vq'opilvar)
*   **ჭამა (*ch'ama* - To eat):** I have eaten -> `მიჭამია` (mich'amia)

**Common Experience Phrases:**
*   **მე საქართველოში არ ვყოფილვარ.** (me Sakartveloshi ar vq'opilvar)
    *   *I have never been to Georgia.*
*   **ხინკალი არასდროს არ მიჭამია.** (khink'ali arasdros ar mich'amia)
    *   *I have never eaten khinkali.*
*   **ეგ ფილმი არ მინახავს.** (eg pilmi ar minakhavs)
    *   *I have not seen that movie.*

> **🗣️ Usage Tip:** Notice how the transitive verbs ("eaten" and "seen") take the `მი- / -მი-` (*mi-* / *-mi-*) prefix[^13] in the first person[^6] (e.g., `მიჭამია` *mich'amia*, `მინახავს` *minakhavs*, or after a preverb as in `დამიწერია` *damits'eria* "I have written"). Remember, the grammar is inverted: "To me, khinkali has never been eaten."

## 5. Practice Dialogue: The Office Gossip

Read this dialogue aloud. Notice how Ana uses `თურმე` (*turme*) and the Perfect tense to share news she just heard, rather than events she witnessed firsthand. 

**ანა (Ana):** დავით, გაიგე? თურმე ნიკა ამერიკაში **წასულა**! (Davit, gaige? Turme Nik'a Amerik'ashi **ts'asula**! - *Davit, did you hear? Apparently Nika **went** to America!*)
**დავითი (Daviti):** მართლა? მე არ ვიცოდი. როდის წავიდა? (Martla? Me ar vitsodi. Rodis ts'avida? - *Really? I didn't know. When did he go?*)
**ანა (Ana):** გუშინ. თურმე იქ ახალი სამსახური **უპოვია**. (Gushin. Turme ik akhali samsakhuri **up'ovia**. - *Yesterday. Apparently he **found** a new job there.*)
**დავითი (Daviti):** რა მაგარია! მე ამერიკაში არასდროს **არ ვყოფილვარ**. (Ra magaria! Me Amerik'ashi arasdros **ar vq'opilvar**. - *How cool! I **have** never **been** to America.*)
**ანა (Ana):** არც მე. თუმცა ნიკა**ს** თურმე ბილეთი უკვე **უყიდია** ჩემთვის! (Arts me. Tumtsa Nik'a**s** turme bilet'i uk've **uq'idia** chemtvis! - *Me neither. However, Nika has apparently already **bought** a ticket for me!*)

### Next Steps
- Write down one thing you have never done in your life using the phrase: `არასდროს არ მიჭამია / მინახავს` (*arasdros ar mich'amia / minakhavs* - I have never eaten/seen...).
- Practice the 3rd person reporting formula: `თურმე [Name]-ს [Verb-უ...ია]` (*turme [Name]-s [Verb-u...ia]*). (E.g., *თურმე ანას პური უყიდია* — *turme Anas p'uri uq'idia* — Apparently Ana bought bread). 
- Listen for the word *turme* in Georgian conversations or TV shows. You will hear it everywhere!

Now that you can smoothly report the unseen past, you are ready to connect your ideas into rich, flowing sentences in **Module 17: Conjunctions & Complex Sentences!**

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Nominative case** (სახელობითი ბრუნვა, *sakhelobiti brunva*) — The basic, dictionary form of a noun, typically used to mark the subject of a sentence.
[^2]: **Present tense** (ახლანდელი დრო, *akhlandeli dro*) — The tense used for actions happening right now or habitually.
[^3]: **Perfect tense** (თურმეობითი მწკრივი, *turmeobiti mts'k'rivi*) — A tense used to describe past events not directly witnessed by the speaker, or actions with ongoing relevance.
[^4]: **Intransitive** (გარდაუვალი, *gardauvali*) — A verb that does not take a direct object.
[^5]: **Future tense** (მყოფადი დრო, *mq'opadi dro*) — The tense used for actions that will happen.
[^6]: **First person** (პირველი პირი, *p'irveli p'iri*) — A grammatical category referring to the speaker.
[^7]: **Dative case** (მიცემითი ბრუნვა, *mitsemiti brunva*) — A grammatical case used for indirect objects, or subjects with certain experiential verbs.
[^8]: **Transitive** (გარდამავალი, *gardamavali*) — A verb that requires a direct object to receive its action.
[^9]: **Past tense** (წარსული დრო, *ts'arsuli dro*) — The tense used for completed actions.
[^10]: **Optative** (კავშირებითი მწკრივი, *k'avshirebiti mts'k'rivi*) — A verb form used to express wishes, possibilities, obligations, or hypothetical situations.
[^11]: **Subject** (ქვემდებარე, *kvemdebare*) — The main person or thing that performs the action or is described in a sentence.
[^12]: **Preverb** (ზმნისწინი, *zmnists'ini*) — A prefix attached to a verb that indicates direction, adds meaning, or changes the verb's aspect.
[^13]: **Prefix** (პრეფიქსი / თავსართი, *p'repiksi* / *tavsarti*) — A grammatical element added to the beginning of a word.
[^14]: **Aorist** (წყვეტილი მწკრივი, *ts'q'vet'ili mts'k'rivi*) — A specific past tense used for completed, simple actions without focusing on their duration.
[^15]: **Verb** (ზმნა, *zmna*) — An action word or word showing a state of being.
