Welcome to Level 3! Up to this point, you have survived using a handful of high-frequency verbs (like *to be*, *to want*, and *to go*). Now, it is time to look at the engine that drives the Georgian language: **The Verb[^17] System**.

Georgian verbs are famously rich, but they are not random. They work like mathematical formulas. A verb is built around a central "root[^19]," and you attach prefixes and suffixes to that root to show exactly *who* is doing the action, *when* it happens, and *to whom*. 

In this module, we will demystify the basic structure of the present tense[^4], learn how to mark the subject[^11], and introduce the concept of the preverb[^13].

---

## 1. Transitive[^9] vs. Intransitive[^5] Verbs (The Big Split)

Before you conjugate a verb, you need to know what kind of verb it is. Georgian divides its verbs into two main categories. This distinction will become crucial when you learn the past tense[^10], so start noticing the difference now:

*   **Transitive Verbs (Action Verbs):** These verbs take a direct object[^3] that receives the action. You are doing something *to* something or someone. 
    *   *Examples:* I build (a house), I eat (an apple), I write (a letter).
*   **Intransitive Verbs (State/Motion Verbs):** These verbs do not take a direct object. The action stays with the subject.
    *   *Examples:* I sleep, I work, I live, I cry.

---

## 2. The Subject Markers (The "v-" Set)

In English, we put pronouns in front of the verb ("I write", "You write"). In Georgian, the verb itself changes to tell you who is doing the action. The independent pronoun[^12] is optional because the verb already contains the person information!

These markers are called the **"v-" set** because the first person[^7] subject is always marked by the prefix `ვ-` (*v-*). 

| Person | Prefix[^14] (Beginning) | Suffix[^15] (Ending) | Notes |
|--------|-------------------------|----------------------|-------|
| **მე** (*me*, I) | **ვ-** (*v-*) | — | Adds `ვ-` (*v-*) to the front. |
| **შენ** (*shen*, You) | *None* | — | Uses the bare present stem[^18]. |
| **ის** (*is*, He/She/It) | *None* | **-ს** (*-s*) *(or **-ა** *-a*)* | Adds a 3rd-person singular suffix (usually `-ს`, *-s*). |
| **ჩვენ** (*chven*, We) | **ვ-** (*v-*) | **-თ** (*-t*) | Same prefix as "I", plus plural[^16] `-თ` (*-t*) at the end. |
| **თქვენ** (*tkven*, You pl./formal) | *None* | **-თ** (*-t*) | Same as singular "You", plus plural `-თ` (*-t*) at the end. |
| **ისინი** (*isini*, They) | *None* | **-ენ** (*-en*) / **-ან** (*-an*) | Replaces `-ს` (*-s*) with a 3rd-person plural ending. |

---

## 3. Conjugating a Transitive Verb: "To Make / To Do"

Let's apply these markers to one of the most useful transitive verbs in the language: **კეთება** (*k'eteba* — to do / to make). Its present tense stem is **-აკეთებ-** (*-ak'eteb-*).

Watch how the prefixes and suffixes wrap around the stem:

| Pronoun | Marker + Stem + Ending | Conjugated Verb | Transliteration | English |
|---------|------------------------|-----------------|-----------------|---------|
| მე (*me*) | **ვ-** (*v-*) + აკეთებ (*ak'eteb*) | **ვაკეთებ** | ***v**ak'eteb* | I do / I make |
| შენ (*shen*) | აკეთებ (*ak'eteb*) | **აკეთებ** | *ak'eteb* | You do / You make |
| ის (*is*) | აკეთებ (*ak'eteb*) + **-ს** (*-s*) | **აკეთებს** | *ak'eteb**s*** | He/She does |
| ჩვენ (*chven*) | **ვ-** (*v-*) + აკეთებ (*ak'eteb*) + **-თ** (*-t*) | **ვაკეთებთ** | ***v**ak'eteb**t*** | We do |
| თქვენ (*tkven*) | აკეთებ (*ak'eteb*) + **-თ** (*-t*) | **აკეთებთ** | *ak'eteb**t*** | You (pl./formal) do |
| ისინი (*isini*) | აკეთებ (*ak'eteb*) + **-ენ** (*-en*) | **აკეთებენ** | *ak'eteb**en*** | They do |

> **🗣️ Usage Example:** 
> **რას აკეთებ?** (*ras ak'eteb?*) — What are you doing?
> **მე საჭმელს ვაკეთებ.** (*me sach'mels vak'eteb*) — I am making food. *(Notice that **საჭმელს**, *sach'mels*, "food", is in the Dative case[^8] ending in `-ს` *-s*, because direct objects of transitive verbs take the Dative case in the present tense!)*

## 4. Conjugating an Intransitive Verb: "To Work"

Now let's look at a common intransitive verb: **მუშაობა** (*mushaoba* — to work). The present tense stem is **-მუშაობ-** (*-mushaob-*). The exact same subject markers apply!

| Pronoun | Marker + Stem + Ending | Conjugated Verb | Transliteration | English |
|---------|------------------------|-----------------|-----------------|---------|
| მე (*me*) | **ვ-** (*v-*) + მუშაობ (*mushaob*) | **ვმუშაობ** | ***v**mushaob* | I work |
| შენ (*shen*) | მუშაობ (*mushaob*) | **მუშაობ** | *mushaob* | You work |
| ის (*is*) | მუშაობ (*mushaob*) + **-ს** (*-s*) | **მუშაობს** | *mushaob**s*** | He/She works |
| ჩვენ (*chven*) | **ვ-** (*v-*) + მუშაობ (*mushaob*) + **-თ** (*-t*) | **ვმუშაობთ** | ***v**mushaob**t*** | We work |
| თქვენ (*tkven*) | მუშაობ (*mushaob*) + **-თ** (*-t*) | **მუშაობთ** | *mushaob**t*** | You (pl./formal) work |
| ისინი (*isini*) | მუშაობ (*mushaob*) + **-ენ** (*-en*) | **მუშაობენ** | *mushaob**en*** | They work |

> **🗣️ Usage Example:**
> **სად მუშაობთ?** (*sad mushaobt?*) — Where do you work? *(Formal)*
> **მე ბანკში ვმუშაობ.** (*me bank'shi vmushaob*) — I work in a bank.

## 5. Object Markers: A Sneak Peek

Verbs don't just show *who* is doing the action; they also show *who is receiving* the action. We will dive deeper into this later, but notice what happens when we change the prefix on the verb:

*   If `ვ-` (*v-*) means "**I** am doing it," the object prefix `მ-` (*m-*) means "It is happening to **me**."
*   The object prefix `გ-` (*g-*) means "It is happening to **you**."

*   **მე ვხედავ.** (*me **v**khedav*) — **I** see (him/her/it).
*   **ის მხედავს.** (*is **m**khedavs*) — He/She sees **me**.
*   **მე გხედავ.** (*me **g**khedav*) — I see **you**.

## 6. A First Encounter with the Preverb (The Time Switch)

When you look up a verb in a Georgian dictionary, you will often see a directional/aspectual prefix attached to the front of the verb, such as **და-** (*da-*), **გა-** (*ga-*), **მო-** (*mo-*), **შე-** (*she-*), or **ჩა-** (*cha-*). This is called a **Preverb**.

Here is the most important concept to grasp about Georgian verbs and time:
**In the present tense, the preverb is stripped off.** 

The preverb acts like a light switch for time. When the switch is OFF (no preverb), the action is unfolding right now in the present tense. When you turn the switch ON (add the preverb to the front), you shift the verb into the **Future Tense[^6]**.

Let's look at "To do/make" (**კეთება**, *k'eteba*). Its lexical preverb is **გა-** (*ga-*):
*   **Present (Switch Off):** **ვაკეთებ** (*vak'eteb*) — I am making/doing it (right now).
*   **Future (Switch On):** **გავაკეთებ** (***ga**vak'eteb*) — I will make/do it.

Let's look at "To write" (**წერა**, *ts'era*). Its lexical preverb is **და-** (*da-*):
*   **Present (Switch Off):** **ვწერ** (*vts'er*) — I am writing.
*   **Future (Switch On):** **დავწერ** (***da**vts'er*) — I will write.

You don't need to memorize every preverb yet, but whenever you learn a new verb, make a mental note of its "future switch."

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Subject marker** (სუბიექტის ნიშანი, *subiekt'is nishani*) — An affix on a verb indicating who is performing the action.
[^2]: **Genitive case** (ნათესაობითი ბრუნვა, *natesaobiti brunva*) — A grammatical case showing possession or relationship, similar to English "of" or "'s".
[^3]: **Direct object** (პირდაპირი დამატება, *p'irdap'iri damat'eba*) — The noun that directly receives the action of a transitive verb.
[^4]: **Present tense** (ახლანდელი დრო, *akhlandeli dro*) — The tense used for actions happening right now or habitually.
[^5]: **Intransitive** (გარდაუვალი, *gardauvali*) — A verb that does not take a direct object.
[^6]: **Future tense** (მყოფადი დრო, *mq'opadi dro*) — The tense used for actions that will happen.
[^7]: **First person** (პირველი პირი, *p'irveli p'iri*) — A grammatical category referring to the speaker.
[^8]: **Dative case** (მიცემითი ბრუნვა, *mitsemiti brunva*) — A grammatical case used for indirect objects, or for direct objects of transitive verbs in the present series.
[^9]: **Transitive** (გარდამავალი, *gardamavali*) — A verb that takes a direct object to receive its action.
[^10]: **Past tense** (წარსული დრო, *ts'arsuli dro*) — The tense used for completed actions.
[^11]: **Subject** (ქვემდებარე, *kvemdebare*) — The main person or thing that performs the action or is described in a sentence.
[^12]: **Pronoun** (ნაცვალსახელი, *natsvalsakheli*) — A word that takes the place of a noun.
[^13]: **Preverb** (ზმნისწინი, *zmnists'ini*) — A prefix attached to a verb that indicates direction, adds lexical meaning, or changes the verb's aspect/tense.
[^14]: **Prefix** (პრეფიქსი / თავსართი, *p'repiksi* / *tavsarti*) — A grammatical element added to the beginning of a word.
[^15]: **Suffix** (სუფიქსი / ბოლოსართი, *supiksi* / *bolosarti*) — A grammatical element added to the end of a word.
[^16]: **Plural** (მრავლობითი რიცხვი, *mravlobiti ritskhvi*) — A grammatical form indicating more than one person or thing.
[^17]: **Verb** (ზმნა, *zmna*) — An action word or word showing a state of being.
[^18]: **Stem** (ფუძე, *pudze*) — The core, basic part of a word before endings are attached.
[^19]: **Root** (ძირი, *dziri*) — The most basic, irreducible part of a word that carries its fundamental meaning.
