Modern Georgian is written in **Mkhedruli** (მხედრული, *mkhedruli*, "knightly" or "secular"), a 33-letter alphabet that emerged in the 10th–11th century. It is one of three historical Georgian writing systems, all of which were inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2016.

Three things to know up front:

- **100% Phonetic.** Each letter corresponds to exactly one sound. Once you learn the alphabet, you can read *any* Georgian word. There are no silent letters, no hidden sounds, and zero spelling exceptions.
- **No Letter Case (Unicase).** Mkhedruli has no uppercase (capital) or lowercase letters. The letters look the same at the beginning of a sentence as they do in the middle.
- **No Diphthongs.** If you see two vowels next to each other, you pronounce both of them clearly. *აეროპორტი* (*aerop'ort'i*, "airport") is `a-e-ro-p'or-t'i`.

## 1. The Vowels (The Easy Part)

Georgian has five vowels. They are clean, crisp, and never change their sound, regardless of where they are in a word. 

| Letter | IPA | English approximation | Practice Word | Meaning |
|--------|-----|-----------------------|---------------|---------|
| `ა` (*a*) | /ɑ/ | f**a**ther | აისი (*aisi*) | *dawn* |
| `ე` (*e*) | /ɛ/ | b**e**d | ენა (*ena*) | *tongue / language* |
| `ი` (*i*) | /i/ | mach**i**ne | ია (*ia*) | *violet* |
| `ო` (*o*) | /ɔ/ | th**ou**ght (RP) | ოთახი (*otakhi*) | *room* |
| `უ` (*u*) | /u/ | f**oo**d | უხილავი (*ukhilavi*) | *invisible* |

> **⚠️ The Golden Rule of Georgian Vowels:** Do not reduce them. In English, unstressed vowels become a lazy "uh" sound. In Georgian, *ბანანი* (*banani*, "banana") has two crisp /ɑ/ sounds and a clean /i/. Never say "buh-na-nuh."

## 2. Navigating the Shapes (Look-Alike Letters)

Before learning the consonant[^1] sounds, you need to train your eyes. Georgian is beautiful, but it relies heavily on loops and hooks. Beginners constantly confuse these groups. Pay close attention to where the loops close and where the stems or tails go:

### The "O" Family
*   `თ` (*t*) — Has a rounded loop on the left with a curved arch extending over the top.
*   `ო` (*o*) — A rounded loop with a small dip or notch at the top.
*   `ძ` (*dz*) — A rounded bottom loop with a tall vertical stem rising on the right.

### The "Hook" Family
*   `ვ` (*v*) — A single top hook curving into a rounded bottom loop.
*   `პ` (*p'*) — Two small arches at the top curving into a bottom hook.
*   `კ` (*k'*) — A short top hook with a deep curved tail dropping below the baseline.
*   `ჰ` (*h*) — Three small stacked arches on the right (like a reversed `3` with an extra loop).

### The "Loop-De-Loop" Family
*   `შ` (*sh*) — A rounded bottom loop topped by two small arches.
*   `წ` (*ts'*) — Two top loops with a long tail dropping below the baseline on the right.
*   `ჭ` (*ch'*) — A tall vertical stem with double top hooks and a rounded bottom loop.

## 3. The Consonants (Familiar Sounds)

These letters represent sounds that already exist in English. You only need to learn their new shapes:

| Letter | Sound | Letter | Sound | Letter | Sound |
|--------|-------|--------|-------|--------|-------|
| `ბ` (*b*) | **b**oat | `ნ` (*n*) | **n**o | `მ` (*m*) | **m**e |
| `გ` (*g*) | **g**o | `რ` (*r*) | rolled **r** | `ს` (*s*) | **s**ee |
| `დ` (*d*) | **d**o | `შ` (*sh*) | **sh**oe | `ჯ` (*j*) | **j**ump |
| `ვ` (*v*) | **v**ine | `ჟ` (*zh*) | mea**s**ure | `ლ` (*l*) | **l**ake |
| `ზ` (*z*) | **z**oo | `ძ` (*dz*) | a**ds** | | |

> **Pronunciation Notes:** 
> `ვ` (*v*) is closer to English /v/ than /w/. **ვაშლი** (*vashli*, "apple") is "vashli," not "washli." 
> `რ` (*r*) is a flapped or trilled /r/ like in Spanish or Italian; never use the retroflex American English "r."

## 4. Aspirated[^2] vs. Ejective[^3] Consonants (The Core Challenge)

Georgian distinguishes between two types of voiceless stops and affricates: Aspirated and Ejective. Getting these right is the difference between asking for a door and asking for the wind.

1. **Voiceless Aspirated:** `ფ` (*p*), `თ` (*t*), `ქ` (*k*), `ც` (*ts*), `ჩ` (*ch*).
   * Produced with a strong puff of air.
2. **Voiceless Ejective:** `პ` (*p'*), `ტ` (*t'*), `კ` (*k'*), `წ` (*ts'*), `ჭ` (*ch'*).
   * Sharp, "popping" sounds made by closing the glottis, with **zero** breath from the lungs.

**The Minimal Pairs:**
| Aspirated (Puff of air) | Ejective (Sharp pop) |
|-------------------------|----------------------|
| <mark>ფ</mark>ური (*puri* — *cow*; everyday word: ძროხა, *dzrokha*) | <mark>პ</mark>ური (*p'uri* — *bread*) |
| <mark>თ</mark>არი (*tari* — *lute [stringed instrument]*) | <mark>ტ</mark>არი (*t'ari* — *handle*) |
| <mark>ქ</mark>არი (*kari* — *wind*) | <mark>კ</mark>არი (*k'ari* — *door*) |
| <mark>ც</mark>ელი (*tseli* — *scythe*) | <mark>წ</mark>ელი (*ts'eli* — *year / waist*) |
| <mark>ჩ</mark>ირი (*chiri* — *dried fruit*) | <mark>ჭ</mark>ირი (*ch'iri* — *plague / trouble*) |

> **The Paper Trick:** Hold a piece of paper an inch from your mouth. Say **ფური** (*puri*, "cow"). The paper should blow back violently. Now say **პური** (*p'uri*, "bread") by closing your throat and popping your lips. The paper should not move at all.

There is one standalone ejective: `ყ` (*q'*). It has no aspirated partner. It is a sharp popping sound made at the very back of the throat, against the uvula. **ყვავილი** (*q'vavili*, "flower") and **ყავა** (*q'ava*, "coffee") are great practice words.

## 5. The Throat Sounds

Georgian has two fricatives articulated at the back of the mouth (velar/uvular). They look intimidating, but they are common in other European languages.

*   `ღ` (*gh*) — A voiced velar/uvular fricative. Like the French "r" in *Paris*, or the Spanish "g" in *agua*. Practice: **ღ**ამე (*ghame*, "night"), **ღ**ვინო (*ghvino*, "wine").
*   `ხ` (*kh*) — A voiceless velar fricative. Like the German "ch" in *Bach*, or the Spanish "j" in *jalapeño*. Practice: **ხ**ინკალი (*khink'ali*, "khinkali dumpling"), **ხ**ილი (*khili*, "fruit").

## 6. Surviving Consonant Clusters

You have probably heard rumors about Georgian consonant clusters. Yes, words like *მწვრთნელი* (*mts'vrtneli* — "trainer") exist. 

As a beginner, you don't need to panic. The trick to pronouncing Georgian clusters is **transitioning smoothly without adding vowels.** In English, we try to insert a phantom "uh" between hard sounds (like saying "s-uh-ports" for sports). In Georgian, you must link them together tightly.

**Practice blending these beginner clusters:**
*   <mark>წყ</mark>ალი (*ts'q'a-li*) — Water
*   <mark>დღ</mark>ეს (*dghes*) — Today
*   <mark>მშ</mark>ვიდობა (*mshvi-do-ba*) — Peace
*   <mark>გვ</mark>არი (*gva-ri*) — Surname

## 7. Word Stress[^5] (Where to put the emphasis)

Georgian does not have heavy, dramatic stress like Russian or English. The stress is very weak and even, meaning you barely emphasize the stressed syllable[^4] and never reduce unstressed vowels.

**The Two Simple Rules:**
1. **2 to 3 Syllable Words:** Stress the **first** syllable.
   * *მად-ლო-ბა* (*madloba*, "thank you") → `MAD-lo-ba`.
   * *ბო-დი-ში* (*bodishi*, "sorry") → `BO-di-shi`.
2. **4+ Syllable Words:** Stress the **antepenultimate** (third from the end) syllable.
   * *გა-მარ-ჯო-ბა* (*gamarjoba*, "hello") → `ga-MAR-jo-ba`. 
   * *სა-ქარ-თვე-ლო* (*sakartvelo*, "Georgia") → `sa-KAR-tve-lo`.

## Reading Practice: International Words

The fastest way to confirm you can decode the alphabet is by reading loan words. 

> *Notice that the English p, t, and k sounds map to Georgian's **ejective** series (`პ` *p'*, `ტ` *t'*, `კ` *k'*), not the aspirated ones. Notice also that consonant-stem nouns all end in `-ი` (*-i*), which is the standard Georgian nominative noun[^6] marker.*

| Georgian | Transliteration | English |
|----------|-----------------|---------|
| ბანკი | *bank'i* | Bank |
| პარკი | *p'ark'i* | Park |
| ინტერნეტი | *int'ernet'i* | Internet |
| ტურისტი | *t'urist'i* | Tourist |
| სპორტი | *sp'ort'i* | Sport |

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Consonant** (თანხმოვანი, *tankhmovani*) — A speech sound that is not a vowel.
[^2]: **Aspirated** (ფშვინვიერი, *pshvinvieri*) — A consonant pronounced with a strong puff of air.
[^3]: **Ejective** (მკვეთრი, *mk'vetri*) — A consonant pronounced with a simultaneous glottal stop and no puff of air.
[^4]: **Syllable** (მარცვალი, *martsvali*) — A unit of pronunciation having one vowel sound.
[^5]: **Stress** (მახვილი, *makhvili*) — The emphasis placed on a particular syllable or word in speech.
[^6]: **Noun** (არსებითი სახელი, *arsebiti sakheli*) — A word that represents a person, place, thing, or idea.
