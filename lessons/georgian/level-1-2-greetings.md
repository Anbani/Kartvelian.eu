Welcome to Module 2! Now that you can read the Mkhedruli script, it's time to start speaking. Georgians are famous for their hospitality, and making the effort to greet people in their native language will instantly win you smiles and goodwill. 

In this lesson, we will comprehensively cover greetings (both formal and street-level), how to introduce yourself and others, and an expanded list of countries and languages.

---

## 1. The Greetings (Formal & Informal)

Georgian greetings are deeply rooted in the country's turbulent history. Rather than simply wishing someone a "good day," Georgians traditionally wish each other victory and peace.

### The Standard Greetings
| Georgian | Transliteration | English Meaning | Literal Meaning |
|----------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| გამარჯობა | *gamarjoba* | Hello | *Victory to you* |
| დილა მშვიდობისა | *dila mshvidobisa* | Good morning | *Morning of peace* |
| საღამო მშვიდობისა | *saghamo mshvidobisa* | Good evening | *Evening of peace* |
| ღამე მშვიდობისა | *ghame mshvidobisa* | Good night | *Night of peace* |
| ნახვამდის | *nakhvamdis* | Goodbye | *Until seeing* |

### Casual & Street Greetings
If you are talking to friends, peers, or people your own age in a casual setting, you will hear these constantly:
| Georgian | Transliteration | Context |
|----------|-----------------|---------|
| სალამი | *salami* | "Hi" or "Peace." Very common among friends. |
| გაგიმარჯოს | *gagimarjos* | "Victory to *you*." The standard singular/informal reply to **გამარჯობა** (*gamarjoba*) (formal/plural: **გაგიმარჯოთ**, *gagimarjot*). |
| გაუმარჯოს | *gaumarjos* | "Cheers!" Primarily a toast cry over wine; literally wishes victory to a third party. |
| დროებით | *droebit* | "For now." A casual way to say "See you later." |
| კარგად | *k'argad* | "Well." The casual, shortened way to say goodbye (literally wishing them well). |

> **🗣️ Cultural Tip:** **გამარჯობა** (*gamarjoba*) is your universal, bulletproof "hello." You can use it at any time of day, with a shopkeeper, a police officer, or a friend.

## 2. "How are you?" & The Spectrum of Replies

Georgian distinguishes between "you" singular[^1]/informal (**შენ** — *shen*) and "you" plural[^3]/formal (**თქვენ** — *tkven*). 

### Asking
*   **როგორ ხარ?** (*rogor khar?*) — How are you? *(Informal)*
*   **როგორ ხართ?** (*rogor khart?*) — How are you? *(Formal / Plural)*
*   **რა ხდება?** (*ra khdeba?*) — What's happening? / What's up? *(Very informal)*
*   **როგორ მიდის საქმეები?** (*rogor midis sakmeebi?*) — How are things going? 

### The Complete Spectrum of Replies
Georgians are honest. If they aren't having a great day, they won't pretend they are. Here is how to answer, from best to worst:

*   **შესანიშნავად** (*shesanishnavad*) — Excellently / Splendidly.
*   **ძალიან კარგად** (*dzalian k'argad*) — Very well.
*   **კარგად ვარ** (*k'argad var*) — I am well. *(The standard polite reply)*
*   **არამიშავს** (*aramishavs*) — Not bad. *(Literally: "Nothing is harming me." Very common!)*
*   **ისე რა** (*ise ra*) — So-so. 
*   **ცუდად ვარ** (*tsudad var*) — I am bad / unwell. *(Only say this if you want a Georgian to immediately try to feed you or take you to a doctor!)*

## 3. Introductions: "What is your name?"

In Georgian, you don't say "My name is...". Instead, the grammar is closer to "I am called..."

*   **რა გქვია?** (*ra gkvia?*) — What is your name? *(Informal)*
*   **რა გქვიათ?** (*ra gkviat?*) — What is your name? *(Formal)*
*   **მე მქვია [Name].** (*me mkvia...*) — My name is [Name].
*   **სასიამოვნოა** (*sasiamovnoa*) — Nice to meet you. *(Literally: "It is pleasant")*

**Introducing someone else:**
*   **ეს არის ჩემი მეგობარი...** (*es aris chemi megobari...*) — This is my friend [Name].
*   **გაიცანით...** (*gaitsanit...*) — Meet [Name]... *(Formal)*

## 4. Countries & Nationalities (The Comprehensive List)

To ask where someone is from: 
*   **საიდან ხარ?** (*saidan khar?*) — Where are you from? *(Informal)*
*   **საიდან ხართ?** (*saidan khart?*) — Where are you from? *(Formal)*

**The Rule:** For most countries, add the suffix[^2] **-ელი** (*-eli*) to the root/stem[^4] of the country's name (dropping the country suffix **-ეთი**, *-eti*, or final vowel **-ა** / **-ია**, *-a* / *-ia*):

| Country (Georgian) | English | Nationality (Add `-ელი`, *-eli*) | English |
|--------------------|---------|----------------------------------|---------|
| ამერიკა (*amerik'a*) | America | ამერიკ**ელი** (*amerik'eli*) | American |
| ბრიტანეთი (*brit'aneti*) | Britain | ბრიტან**ელი** (*brit'aneli*) | British |
| გერმანია (*germania*) | Germany | გერმან**ელი** (*germaneli*) | German |
| ესპანეთი (*esp'aneti*) | Spain | ესპან**ელი** (*esp'aneli*) | Spanish |
| იტალია (*it'alia*) | Italy | იტალი**ელი** (*it'alieli*) | Italian |
| პოლონეთი (*p'oloneti*) | Poland | პოლონ**ელი** (*p'oloneli*) | Polish |
| უკრაინა (*uk'raina*) | Ukraine | უკრაინ**ელი** (*uk'raineli*) | Ukrainian |
| ჩინეთი (*chineti*) | China | ჩინ**ელი** (*chineli*) | Chinese |
| იაპონია (*iap'onia*) | Japan | იაპონ**ელი** (*iap'oneli*) | Japanese |
| ავსტრალია (*avst'ralia*) | Australia | ავსტრალი**ელი** (*avst'ralieli*) | Australian |

**The Exceptions:** Some historically neighboring or well-known countries derive the country name from the ethnonym rather than adding **-ელი** (*-eli*):

| Country (Georgian) | English | Nationality (Exception) | English |
|--------------------|---------|-------------------------|---------|
| საფრანგეთი (*saprangeti*) | France | ფრანგი (*prangi*) | French |
| რუსეთი (*ruseti*) | Russia | რუსი (*rusi*) | Russian |
| თურქეთი (*turketi*) | Turkey | თურქი (*turki*) | Turkish |
| სომხეთი (*somkheti*) | Armenia | სომეხი (*somekhi*) | Armenian |
| საბერძნეთი (*saberdzneti*) | Greece | ბერძენი (*berdzeni*) | Greek |

> **👑 The Biggest Exception:** The word for a Georgian person is **ქართველი** (*kartveli*). The country of Georgia is called **საქართველო** (*sakartvelo*) — literally "the land of the Kartvelians."

**To state your nationality:**
*   **მე ვარ კანადელი.** (*me var k'anadeli*) — I am Canadian.
*   **მე ვარ ფრანგი.** (*me var prangi*) — I am French.
*   **ის არის ქართველი.** (*is aris kartveli*) — He/She is Georgian.

## 5. Languages: "Do you speak...?"

When talking about languages, Georgian uses the suffix **-ული** (*-uli*) or **-ური** (*-uri*). Furthermore, Georgians don't ask if you *speak* a language; they ask if you *know* (**იცი** — *itsi*) the language.

| Language (Georgian) | Transliteration | English |
|---------------------|-----------------|---------|
| ინგლისური | *inglisuri* | English |
| ქართული | *kartuli* | Georgian |
| ფრანგული | *pranguli* | French |
| გერმანული | *germanuli* | German |
| რუსული | *rusuli* | Russian |
| ესპანური | *esp'anuri* | Spanish |

**Asking & Answering:**
*   **ქართული იცი?** (*kartuli itsi?*) — Do you speak (know) Georgian? *(Informal)*
*   **კი, ცოტა ვიცი.** (*k'i, tsot'a vitsi*) — Yes, I know a little.
*   **არა, არ ვიცი.** (*ara, ar vitsi*) — No, I don't know it.
*   **ინგლისური იცით?** (*inglisuri itsit?*) — Do you speak (know) English? *(Formal)*

## 6. Putting It All Together: Practice Dialogues

### Dialogue A: Casual (Meeting at a cafe)
**ანა** (*Ana*): სალამი! როგორ ხარ? (*Salami! Rogor khar?* — *Hi! How are you?*)
**ტომი** (*T'omi*): არამიშავს, შენ? (*Aramishavs, shen?* — *Not bad, and you?*)
**ანა** (*Ana*): კარგად, მადლობა. რა გქვია? (*K'argad, madloba. Ra gkvia?* — *Well, thanks. What is your name?*)
**ტომი** (*T'omi*): მე მქვია ტომი. სასიამოვნოა. (*Me mkvia T'omi. Sasiamovnoa.* — *My name is Tom. Nice to meet you.*)
**ანა** (*Ana*): საიდან ხარ, ტომ? (*Saidan khar, T'om?* — *Where are you from, Tom?*)
**ტომი** (*T'omi*): მე ვარ ამერიკელი. შენ ქართველი ხარ, ხომ? (*Me var amerik'eli. Shen kartveli khar, khom?* — *I am American. You are Georgian, right?*)
**ანა** (*Ana*): კი, ქართველი ვარ. (*K'i, kartveli var.* — *Yes, I am Georgian.*)

### Dialogue B: Formal (Meeting an older neighbor)
**ტურისტი** (*t'urist'i*, Tourist): გამარჯობა. (*Gamarjoba.* — *Hello.*)
**მეზობელი** (*mezobeli*, Neighbor): გაგიმარჯოთ. როგორ ხართ? (*Gagimarjot. Rogor khart?* — *Hello [to you]. How are you?*) *[Note: **გაგიმარჯოთ** (*gagimarjot*) is the formal/plural reply to **გამარჯობა** (*gamarjoba*); **გაგიმარჯოს** (*gagimarjos*) is informal/singular.]*
**ტურისტი** (*t'urist'i*, Tourist): დიდი მადლობა, კარგად. თქვენ? (*Didi madloba, k'argad. Tkven?* — *Thank you very much, well. And you?*)
**მეზობელი** (*mezobeli*, Neighbor): მადლობა ღმერთს, კარგად. საიდან ხართ? (*Madloba ghmerts, k'argad. Saidan khart?* — *Thank God, well. Where are you from?*)
**ტურისტი** (*t'urist'i*, Tourist): მე გერმანელი ვარ. ქართული ცოტა ვიცი. (*Me germaneli var. Kartuli tsot'a vitsi.* — *I am German. I know a little Georgian.*)
**მეზობელი** (*mezobeli*, Neighbor): ძალიან კარგია! (*Dzalian k'argia!* — *Very good!*)

### Next Steps
- Memorize the casual greetings (**სალამი**, *salami*; **არამიშავს**, *aramishavs*).
- Find your home country and figure out your exact nationality and language in Georgian.
- Read both dialogues aloud to practice the rhythm of the language.

When you feel comfortable introducing yourself, move on to **Module 3: Basic Nouns & Plurals!**

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Singular** (მხოლობითი რიცხვი, *mkholobiti ritskhvi*) — A grammatical form indicating one person or thing.
[^2]: **Suffix** (სუფიქსი / ბოლოსართი, *supiksi* / *bolosarti*) — A grammatical element added to the end of a word.
[^3]: **Plural** (მრავლობითი რიცხვი, *mravlobiti ritskhvi*) — A grammatical form indicating more than one person or thing.
[^4]: **Stem** (ფუძე, *pudze*) — The core, basic part of a word before endings are attached.
