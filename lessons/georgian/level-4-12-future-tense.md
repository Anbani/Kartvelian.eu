Welcome to Level 4! You are now entering the realm of time travel. 

In many languages, learning the future tense[^2] means memorizing an entirely new set of conjugations and endings. Georgian, however, gives you a massive shortcut. If you know how to conjugate a verb[^9] in the present tense[^1] (which you learned in Module 9), you already know how to conjugate it in the future. 

The secret lies in a tiny, magical prefix[^7] called the **Preverb[^6]**.

---

## 1. The Preverb: The Time Machine Switch

As we briefly discussed in Level 3, almost every Georgian verb has an assigned **Preverb** (ზმნისწინი - *zmnists'ini*). 

In the present tense, this preverb is stripped off. To create the future tense, you simply **snap the preverb onto the front of the present tense verb.** That's it! The subject[^4] markers (`ვ-` *v-*, `-თ` *-t*, `-ს` *-s*) remain exactly the same.

**The Formula:** 
`[Assigned Preverb] + [Present Tense Verb] = [Future Tense]`

---

## 2. Common Preverbs & Regular Verbs

There are several preverbs in Georgian (e.g., **და-** *da-*, **გა-** *ga-*, **შე-** *she-*, **ჩა-** *cha-*, **მო-** *mo-*, **წა-** *ts'a-*). While they originally indicated physical direction (like up, down, in, out), for the sake of conjugation[^3], you just need to memorize which preverb belongs to which verb.

Let's look at three highly common regular verbs and their designated preverbs:

### A. To Do / To Make (Preverb: `გა-` *ga-*)
| Pronoun[^5] | Present Tense | Add the Preverb | Future Tense | English |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|--------------|---------|
| მე (*me*) | ვაკეთებ (vak'eteb)| **გა** + ვაკეთებ (*ga* + *vak'eteb*) | **გავაკეთებ** (gavak'eteb)| I will do/make |
| შენ (*shen*) | აკეთებ (ak'eteb) | **გა** + აკეთებ (*ga* + *ak'eteb*) | **გააკეთებ** (gaak'eteb)| You will do |
| ის (*is*) | აკეთებს (ak'etebs)| **გა** + აკეთებს (*ga* + *ak'etebs*) | **გააკეთებს** (gaak'etebs)| He/She will do |

### B. To Write (Preverb: `და-` *da-*)
| Pronoun | Present Tense | Add the Preverb | Future Tense | English |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|--------------|---------|
| ჩვენ (*chven*) | ვწერთ (vts'ert) | **და** + ვწერთ (*da* + *vts'ert*) | **დავწერთ** (davts'ert) | We will write |
| თქვენ (*tkven*) | წერთ (ts'ert) | **და** + წერთ (*da* + *ts'ert*) | **დაწერთ** (dats'ert) | You (pl) will write|
| ისინი (*isini*) | წერენ (ts'eren) | **და** + წერენ (*da* + *ts'eren*) | **დაწერენ** (dats'eren) | They will write |

### C. To Eat (Preverb: `შე-` *she-*)
| Pronoun | Present Tense | Add the Preverb | Future Tense | English |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|--------------|---------|
| მე (*me*) | ვჭამ (vch'am) | **შე** + ვჭამ (*she* + *vch'am*) | **შევჭამ** (shevch'am) | I will eat |
| ის (*is*) | ჭამს (ch'ams) | **შე** + ჭამს (*she* + *ch'ams*) | **შეჭამს** (shech'ams) | He/She will eat |

## 3. The Vital Irregulars: To Be, To Go, To Drink

While the preverb system works beautifully for most verbs, the most fundamental verbs of human existence are often irregular in any language. You must memorize these three exceptions.

### 1. To Be (ყოფნა, *q'opna*)
The verb "to be" changes its stem[^10] entirely in the future. 
*   მე ვარ (*me var* - I am) ➔ **მე ვიქნები** (me viknebi - I will be)
*   ის არის (*is aris* - He/She is) ➔ **ის იქნება** (is ikneba - He/She will be)

### 2. To Go (წასვლა, *ts'asvla*)
The directional present tense verbs (**მივდივარ** *mivdivar* / **მოვდივარ** *movdivar*) switch to a shorter, sleeker root[^11] in the future (**წავალ** *ts'aval* "I will go" / **მოვალ** *moval* "I will come").
*   მე მივდივარ (*me mivdivar* - I am going) ➔ **მე წავალ** (me ts'aval - I will go)
*   ის მიდის (*is midis* - He/She is going) ➔ **ის წავა** (is ts'ava - He/She will go)
*   ჩვენ მივდივართ (*chven mivdivart* - We are going) ➔ **ჩვენ წავალთ** (chven ts'avalt - We will go)

### 3. To Drink (სმა, *sma* / დალევა, *daleva*)
"To eat" is regular (`შევჭამ`, *shevch'am*), but "to drink" switches to a different root in the future.
*   მე ვსვამ (*me vsvam* - I am drinking) ➔ **მე დავლევ** (me davlev - I will drink)
*   ის სვამს (*is svams* - He/She is drinking) ➔ **ის დალევს** (is dalevs - He/She will drink)

## 4. Planning Ahead: Future Time Words

To sound natural, you need to anchor your future verbs with time expressions.

| Georgian | Transliteration | English |
|----------|-----------------|---------|
| **ხვალ** | khval | Tomorrow |
| **ზეგ** | zeg | The day after tomorrow |
| **მომავალ კვირას** | momaval k'viras | Next week *(literally: future week-to)* |
| **მომავალ წელს** | momaval ts'els | Next year |
| **ამ შაბათ-კვირას**| am shabat-k'viras| This weekend *(literally: this Saturday-Sunday)* |
| **მერე / შემდეგ** | mere / shemdeg | Later / Then |

## 5. Putting It Together: Weekend Plans

Read this dialogue aloud. Notice how the speakers establish that they are talking about the future using time words, and pay attention to the preverbs attached to the verbs.

**ნიკა (Nik'a):** რას აკეთებ ამ შაბათ-კვირას? (Ras ak'eteb am shabat-k'viras? - *What are you doing this weekend?*)
**ანა (Ana):** შაბათს სახლში **ვიქნები** და **დავწერ** წერილს. კვირას მეგობართან **წავალ**. შენ? (Shabats sakhlshi **viknebi** da **davts'er** ts'erils. K'viras megobartan **ts'aval**. Shen? - *On Saturday I **will be** at home and I **will write** a letter. On Sunday I **will go** to a friend's. You?*)
**ნიკა (Nik'a):** მე და ჩემი ძმა რესტორანში **წავალთ**. ჩვენ ხინკალს **შევჭამთ** და ლუდს **დავლევთ**. (Me da chemi dzma rest'oranshi **ts'avalt**. Chven khink'als **shevch'amt** da luds **davlevt**. - *My brother and I **will go** to a restaurant. We **will eat** khinkali and we **will drink** beer.*)
**ანა (Ana):** ძალიან კარგი! მერე რას **გააკეთებთ**? (Dzalian k'argi! Mere ras **gaak'etebt**? - *Very good! What **will you (pl) do** then/later?*)
**ნიკა (Nik'a):** მერე სახლში **წავალთ**. (Mere sakhlshi **ts'avalt**. - *Then we **will go** home.*)

### Next Steps
- Write down three things you will do tomorrow. (Start the sentence with `ხვალ მე...` *khval me...* and don't forget your preverbs!)
- Memorize the future forms of "To be" (`ვიქნები` *viknebi*) and "To go" (`წავალ` *ts'aval*). You will use them constantly.
- Look up a new verb, find its preverb, and try conjugating it in the future tense.

When you can confidently schedule your future, you are ready to tackle the ghosts of the past in **Module 13: The Aorist[^8] & The Ergative (Narrative) Case!**

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Present tense** (ახლანდელი დრო, *akhlandeli dro*) — The tense used for actions happening right now or habitually.
[^2]: **Future tense** (მყოფადი დრო, *mq'opadi dro*) — The tense used for actions that will happen.
[^3]: **Conjugation** (უღვლილება, *ughvlileba*) — The process of changing a verb's form to indicate tense, person, or number.
[^4]: **Subject** (ქვემდებარე, *kvemdebare*) — The main person or thing that performs the action or is described in a sentence.
[^5]: **Pronoun** (ნაცვალსახელი, *natsvalsakheli*) — A word that takes the place of a noun.
[^6]: **Preverb** (ზმნისწინი, *zmnists'ini*) — A prefix attached to a verb that indicates direction, adds meaning, or changes the verb's aspect.
[^7]: **Prefix** (პრეფიქსი / თავსართი, *p'repiksi* / *tavsarti*) — A grammatical element added to the beginning of a word.
[^8]: **Aorist** (წყვეტილი მწკრივი, *ts'q'vet'ili mts'k'rivi*) — A specific past tense used for completed, simple actions without focusing on their duration.
[^9]: **Verb** (ზმნა, *zmna*) — An action word or word showing a state of being.
[^10]: **Stem** (ფუძე, *pudze*) — The core, basic part of a word before endings are attached.
[^11]: **Root** (ძირი, *dziri*) — The most basic, irreducible part of a word that carries its fundamental meaning.
