Welcome to Laz (ლაზური / *Lazuri*)!

Laz is a Kartvelian language primarily spoken along the Black Sea coast. Unlike its sister languages (Georgian, Megrelian, and Svan), the Laz community is split across an international border. This has resulted in the language being written in two very different scripts:

1.  **Mkhedruli (მხედრული, *mkhedruli*):** Used by the Laz community in **Georgia**.
2.  **Latin-based (Laz Alfabesi):** Used by the Laz community in **Türkiye**. The standard Latin alphabet was devised in 1984 by Fahri Lazoğlu and Wolfgang Feurstein and has 35 letters.

To be a true student of Laz, you must understand how these two systems map onto each other.

---

## 1. The Sound System

Laz shares the same phonological DNA as Georgian and Megrelian. This means it includes:
*   **Glottalized/Ejective[^2] Consonants:** Sharp, popping sounds. In Mkhedruli they are პ, ტ, კ, წ, ჭ (and ყ in eastern dialects); in Laz Latin they carry a caron or a special letter: `p̌`, `ť`, `ǩ`, `ǯ`, `ç̌` (and `q`).
*   **Three letters for "ts"-type sounds:** Laz Latin uses `ʒ` (ც), `ž` (ძ) and `ǯ` (წ) instead of letter pairs.

## 3. Key Differences in the Latin Script

If you are used to the Turkish alphabet, pay close attention to these specific Laz adaptations in the Latin system:

*   **The Caron ( ˇ ):** This marks an **ejective** (glottalized) sound. For example, `k` is a regular 'k', but `ǩ` is the sharp, popping Georgian-style `კ` (*k'*). The same goes for `p̌` (პ), `ť` (ტ) and `ç̌` (ჭ).
*   **The Letters `ʒ`, `ž`, `ǯ`:** These stand for ც (*ts*), ძ (*dz*) and წ (*ts'*). Some school materials in Türkiye use the letter pairs `ts`, `dz` and `tz` for these sounds instead.
*   **The Letter 'C':** In Laz Latin, **c** is pronounced like the 'j' in "jump" (Georgian `ჯ`, *j*).
*   **The Letter 'Ç':** Just like in Turkish, this is 'ch', but remember that **ç̌** (with the caron) is the ejective version (`ჭ`, *ch'*).
*   **The Letter 'X':** This represents the raspy Georgian `ხ` (*kh*) sound.
*   **The Letter 'F':** `f` (ჶ) appears only in borrowed words.

---

## 4. The Letter `ყ` / `Q q`

The 35th letter is **Q q**, written **ყ** in Mkhedruli. It is a throaty "k" that survives only in the **eastern dialects**; in the central dialects it has become `k`, and in the western dialects it is mostly dropped.

**Practice reading:**
*   **ყუჯი** / **quci** — ear
*   **ყა** / **qa** — branch

## 5. Summary and Reading Practice

Laz speakers in Georgia usually read Mkhedruli and Laz speakers in Türkiye usually read the Latin alphabet, so the same word can look completely different on the page.

**Try reading these common words in both scripts (Mkhedruli / Laz Latin):**

1.  **I and You:**
    *   მა / ma
    *   სი / si
2.  **Name:**
    *   ჯოხო / coxo
3.  **Water:**
    *   წკაი / ǯǩai
4.  **How are you?**
    *   მუჭ ორე? / Muç̌ ore?

### Next Steps
- If you know Mkhedruli, practice writing the Laz Latin equivalents of the ejective letters (`p̌, ť, ǩ, ǯ, ç̌` and, in the east, `q`).
- Memorize the sound of **c** as "j" and **x** as "kh".
- Read the word for water (**წკაი / ǯǩai**) out loud, focusing on the popping `ǯ` and `ǩ`.

When you can navigate both scripts, you are ready for **Module 2: Greetings in Laz!**

[^2]: **Ejective** (მკვეთრი, *mk'vetri*) — A consonant pronounced with a simultaneous glottal stop and no puff of air.
