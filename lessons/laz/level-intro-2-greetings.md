Welcome to Module 2! Now that you can navigate the two scripts of the Laz language, it’s time to start using them to speak.

Laz is a language of the coast and the mountains. Whether you are in Sarpi (Georgia) or Arhavi (Türkiye), knowing these basic greetings will immediately help you connect with the Lazuri-speaking community. Because of the geographic split, you will see both **Mkhedruli** and **Latin** scripts used below.

The phrases in this lesson come from the Laz course of the Turkish Ministry of Education (MEB), and the single words from the *Georgian–Megrelian–Laz–Svan–English Dictionary* (Tbilisi 2015). Laz has several dialects, so you will meet other forms too; where a form belongs to one dialect, we say so.

---

## 1. Essential Greetings

| Mkhedruli | Latin | English Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ხელა კაობა!** | **Xela ǩaoba!** | Hello! (greeting used in the MEB course) |
| **გამარჯობა** | **Gamarcoba** | Hello (as used in Georgia, the same word as Georgian) |
| **სელამი** | **Selami** | Greeting, salute |
| **კაითენ** | **Ǩaiten** | Goodbye |
| **კაითე ბიძირათ!** | **Ǩaite bidzirat!** | Goodbye! (farewell used in the MEB course) |

The MEB course writes letter pairs such as `dz` (as in *bidzirat*) where the Lazoğlu alphabet uses a single letter.

Fixed phrases for "good morning" or "good night" are not recorded in our sources, so we teach the words instead:

*   **ჭუმანი** (**ç̌umani**) — tomorrow; also morning
*   **სერი** (**seri**) — night
*   **ნდღა** (**ndğa**) — day

## 2. "How are you?" & Wellness

Laz uses the word **მუჭო** (*muç̌o*) for "how." The MEB course writes the question below with the short form *muç̌*.

### Asking
*   **მუჭ ორე?** (**Muç̌ ore?**) — How are you?

When asking several people, the verb "you are" takes its plural[^1] form, for example **რეთ** (*ret*, "you all are") in the Vitse–Arkabi dialect.

### Replying
*   **ვროსი ბორე.** (**Vrosi bore.**) — I am well.

Useful words for your own answers:
*   **კაი** (**ǩai**) — good; well
*   **პატი** (**p̌aťi**) — bad
*   **დიდო** (**dido**) — very
*   **ვროსი** (**vrosi**) — good, fine; well (Vizha and Artasheni dialects)

"I am" also differs by dialect: you will see both **ვორე** (*vore*) and **ბორე** (*bore*). The MEB course uses both; *bore* is the form of the Vitse–Arkabi dialect.

## 3. Introductions: "What is your name?"

In Laz, "name" is **ჯოხო** (*coxo*). Remember: in the Latin script, **c** is pronounced like the **j** in "jump." Some dialects say **ჲოხო** (*yoxo*).

*   **სი მუ ჯეგოზუნ?** (**Si mu cegozun?**) — What is your name?
*   **მა [Name] ჯემოზუნ.** (**Ma [Name] cemozun.**) — My name is [Name].

## 4. Basic Courtesies (The Essentials)

These words often differ depending on which side of the border you are on.

| Mkhedruli | Latin | English | Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ჰო / ხო** | **Ho / Xo** | Yes | |
| **ვარ** | **Var** | No | |
| **საღოლ** | **Sağol** | Thank you | Borrowed from Turkish |
| **მადლობა** | **Madloba** | Thanks | Same word as Georgian |
| **ბოდიში** | **Bodişi** | Sorry / Excuse me | Same word as Georgian |

A native Laz word for "please" is not recorded in our sources, so we leave it out.

## 5. Vocabulary Spotlight: I and You

Laz pronouns are very similar to Megrelian.

*   **მა** (**ma**) — I
*   **სი** (**si**) — You
*   **ჰიმ** (**him**) — He / She *(as used in the MEB course)*
*   **იჲა** (**iya**) — He / She / It *(form given in the 2015 dictionary)*
*   **ჰეჲა** (**heya**) — He / She / It *(Vitse–Arkabi dialect)*

## 6. Practice Dialogue

Try reading this dialogue in both scripts. It is the model dialogue from the MEB course: two people greet each other, exchange names and say where they are from.

**თენა (Tena):** ხელა კაობა! მა თენა ჯემოზუნ. სი მუ ჯეგოზუნ? (**Xela ǩaoba! Ma Tena cemozun. Si mu cegozun?**)
*Hello! My name is Tena. What is your name?*
**თეონა (Teona):** მა თეონა ჯემოზუნ. სი სონური ორე? (**Ma Teona cemozun. Si sonuri ore?**)
*My name is Teona. Where are you from?*
**თენა (Tena):** მა ათინური ვორე. (**Ma Atinuri vore.**)
*I am from Atina.*

### Next Steps
- Practice asking how someone is: **მუჭ ორე?** (*Muç̌ ore?*).
- Memorize the Laz word for "No": **ვარ** (*Var*).
- Write your own name in the intro formula: **მა ... ჯემოზუნ** (*Ma ... cemozun*).

Once you are comfortable with these greetings, you have completed the current introductory modules for Laz!
