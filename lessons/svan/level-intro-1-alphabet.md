Welcome to Svan (ლუშნუ ნინ, *Lushnu nin*)!

Svan is the most distant member of the Kartvelian family: it was the first branch to split off, long before Georgian and Megrelian–Laz went their separate ways. It is spoken high in the Caucasus mountains of Upper and Lower Svaneti, and its dialects differ noticeably from valley to valley.

**Which Svan this course teaches.** Every Svan word in these lessons follows one dialect, **Upper Bal**, mainly as spoken in **Ushguli**, exactly as recorded in the *Georgian–Megrelian–Laz–Svan–English Dictionary* (Kurdadze, Shonia, Tandilava, Nizharadze, Tbilisi 2015). If you hear a different form in Mestia or in Lower Svaneti, it is not necessarily wrong; it is another dialect.

Svan is primarily a spoken language, but when it is written, it uses the **Mkhedruli** script with diacritics that mark its extra vowel[^2] sounds, plus a few additional letters.

---

## 1. The Core Foundation

Svan uses the 33 standard letters of the Georgian alphabet. What it adds is mostly on the vowels: Upper Bal has more vowel sounds than Georgian, and the dictionary writes them by putting marks above the five ordinary vowels. The single letter **ა** (*a*), for example, appears in four forms:

| Form | Transliteration | Svan Example | Meaning |
|------|-----------------|--------------|---------|
| **ა** | a | ალ (al) | That |
| **ა̈** | ä | ბა̈ჩ (bäch) | Stone |
| **ა̄** | ā | ა̄დუ (ādu) | Yes |
| **ა̄̈** | ǟ | ჩა̄̈ჟ (chǟzh) | Horse |

### The Diacritic Toolkit:
*   **The Umlaut ( ̈ ):** Used to "front" a vowel (like in German).
*   **The Macron ( ̄ ):** Used to mark a long vowel (the sound is held longer).
*   **Both together ( ̄̈ ):** A long fronted vowel, as in **ჩა̄̈ჟ** (*chǟzh*, "horse").

## 2. The Expanded Svan Vowels

Here are the extensions you will meet most often in Upper Bal words.

### Fronted Vowels (Umlauts)
| Letter | IPA | Sound | Svan Example | Meaning |
|--------|-----|-------|--------------|---------|
| **ა̈** | /æ/ | 'a' in **cat** | ბ**ა̈**ჩ (bäch) | Stone |
| **ო̈** | /ø/ | 'eu' in French **peu** | ფ**ო̈**ლ (pöl) | Hoof |
| **უ̈** | /y/ | 'u' in French **lune** | კ**უ̈**ლთხი (k'ültkhi) | High |

### Long Vowels (Macrons)
Upper Bal keeps long and short vowels apart, and the dictionary marks every long one. Learn each word together with its length mark.
| Letter | Sound | Svan Example | Meaning |
|--------|-------|--------------|---------|
| **ა̄** | Long 'ah' | მ**ა̄**რე (māre) | Man |
| **ე̄** | Long 'eh' | ლ**ე̄**თ (lēt) | Night |
| **ი̄** | Long 'ee' | **ი̄**სნაჳ (īsnaw) | Dream |
| **ო̄** | Long 'oh' | ყ**ო̄**რ (q'ōr) | Door |
| **უ̄** | Long 'oo' | ტ**უ̄**ნუ (t'ūnu) | Pot |

## 3. Extra Letters

Besides the 33 standard letters, the dictionary's Upper Bal spellings use four letters that standard Georgian does not use:

*   **ჲ** (*y*, /j/): the 'y' of English *yes*. *Example:* **ჲა̈რ** (*yär*) — "who?"
*   **ჳ** (*w*, /w/): the 'w' of English *wet*, often after a consonant. *Example:* **მაჴჳა̄მ** (*maqhwām*) — "thanks"
*   **ჴ** (*qh*, /qʰ/): a *q* sound made far back in the throat, with a puff of air. *Example:* the same **მაჴჳა̄მ** (*maqhwām*).
*   **ჷ** (*ə*, /ə/): a short, neutral vowel, like the 'a' in English *about*. *Example:* **კჷლ** (*k'əl*) — "key"

The ejective[^1] consonants **კ, პ, ტ, წ, ჭ, ყ** work just as they do in Georgian, as in **კოჯ** (*k'oj*, "rock") and **ყო̄რ** (*q'ōr*, "door").

---

## 4. Reading the Marks

When you meet a new Svan word, read it in this order:
1.  **The base letter** tells you which vowel you start from (**ა, ე, ი, ო, უ**).
2.  **Two dots** ( ̈ ) move that vowel to the front of the mouth: **ა̈** sounds close to the 'a' of *cat*.
3.  **A bar** ( ̄ ) tells you to hold the vowel longer.
4.  **Both** ( ̄̈ ) mean a fronted vowel that is also long, as in **ჩა̄̈ჟ** (*chǟzh*).

## 5. First Reading Practice: Essential Svan

Try reading these basic words using your knowledge of Mkhedruli and the new Svan vowel marks.

| Svan | Transliteration | English | Georgian Equivalent |
|------|-----------------|---------|---------------------|
| ხოჩა | khocha | Good | კარგი (k'argi) |
| ეზა̈რდ | ezärd | Well | კარგად (k'argad) |
| დჲა̈რ | dyär | Bread | პური (p'uri) |
| ლიზჳებ | lizweb | To eat | ჭამა (ch'ama) |
| მი | mi | I | მე (me) |
| ჲა̈რ? | yär? | Who? | ვინ? (vin?) |
| ხოჩა ლადეღ! | khocha ladegh! | Hello! | გამარჯობა! (gamarjoba!) |

> **🗣️ Pronunciation Note:** In the word **ნა̈ბოზ** (*näboz*, "evening"), make sure your mouth is open wide on **ა̈** (*ä*) like you are saying "apple," not the flat "a" of "father."

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Ejective** (მკვეთრი, *mk'vetri*) — A consonant pronounced with a simultaneous glottal stop and no puff of air.
[^2]: **Vowel** (ხმოვანი, *khmovani*) — A speech sound made with your mouth open, not blocked by your teeth, tongue, or lips.
