Svan is a deeply oral language, so while the words below are written in Mkhedruli, focus on the sounds, especially the long vowels and umlauts we covered in Module 1. As in Module 1, every Svan form here is **Upper Bal** (mainly Ushguli), as recorded in the *Georgian–Megrelian–Laz–Svan–English Dictionary* (Tbilisi 2015). We only teach a phrase when the dictionary records it as a phrase; everything else appears as single words.

Unlike Georgian, which uses a wish for "victory" (**გამარჯობა**, *gamarjoba*) as its primary greeting, the Svan greeting is built on the word for "good" (**ხოჩა**, *khocha*).

---

## 1. The Standard Greeting

The word **ხოჩა** (*khocha*) means "good." Paired with **ლადეღ** (*ladegh*, "day"), it gives the everyday Svan hello, and the same root[^3] reappears in the Svan goodbye (Section 4).

| Svan | Transliteration | English Meaning |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **ხოჩა ლადეღ!** | *khocha ladegh* | Hello! (lit. "Good day") |

### Times of Day
These are the words for the parts of the day. Learn them as vocabulary: the dictionary does not record "good morning" or "good night" as set greetings, so we do not invent them.

| Svan | Transliteration | English Meaning |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **ჰამ** / **ძინა̈რ** | *ham* / *dzinär* | Morning |
| **ლადეღ** | *ladegh* | Day |
| **ნა̈ბოზ** | *näboz* | Evening |
| **ლე̄თ** | *lēt* | Night |

> **🗣️ Pronunciation Note:** In **ძინა̈რ** (*dzinär*) and **ნა̈ბოზ** (*näboz*), the letter **ა̈** (*ä*) has the vowel sound of English "cat" (/æ/). In **ხოჩა** (*khocha*), **ხ** (*kh*) is the raspy velar fricative (/x/) also found in Georgian. In **ლე̄თ** (*lēt*), hold the **ე̄** (*ē*) a little longer.

---

## 2. "How?" & Describing How Things Are

The dictionary does not record a set phrase for "How are you?", so this module does not teach one. These are the building blocks you will need once you start on Svan sentences:

*   **იმჟი** (*imzhi*) — How?
*   **ხოჩა** (*khocha*) / **ეზა̈რ** (*ezär*) — Good.
*   **ეზა̈რდ** (*ezärd*) — Well.
*   **ხოლა** (*khola*) — Bad.

---

## 3. Introductions

Full introduction sentences ("What is your name?", "My name is…") are not recorded in the dictionary, so here are the words they are built from:

*   **ჟახე** (*zhakhe*) — Name.
*   **ჲა̈რ** (*yär*) — Who?
*   **მა̈ჲ** (*mäy*) — What? *(also in **მა̈ჲკა̈ლიბ**, mäykälib, "what kind of?")*
*   **ისგუ** (*isgu*) — Your *(one person)*.

**Key Pronouns:**
*   **მი** (*mi*) — I
*   **სი** (*si*) — You *(one person)*. **სი მა̈ჲ ჯაკუჲ?** (*si mäy jakuy?*) — What do you want?
*   **სგა̈ჲ** (*sgäy*) — You *(plural[^1], "you all")*
*   **ეჯა** / **ეჯი** (*eja* / *eji*) — He / She / It

---

## 4. Saying Goodbye

The dictionary gives one Svan farewell:

*   **ხოჩამდუ ხა̈რი!** (*khochamdu khäri!*) — Goodbye!

---

## 5. Basic Courtesies

Even if your Svan is limited, these expressions will open many doors (and possibly get you invited for some kubdari, **კუბდარი** (*k'ubdari*) in Georgian, the Svan spiced meat pie).

| Svan | Transliteration | English |
|------|-----------------|---------|
| **იჳასუ ხა̈რი** / **მაჴჳა̈მ** | *iwasu khäri* / *maqhwäm* | Thank you |
| **მაჴჳა̄მ** | *maqhwām* | Thanks |
| **ა̄დუ** | *ādu* | Yes |
| **დე̄მა** / **დე̄სა** / **მა̄მა** | *dēma* / *dēsa* / *māma* | No |
| **ბოდიშ** | *bodish* | Apology / Sorry *(same word as Georgian **ბოდიში**, bodishi)* |

---

## 6. Practice Dialogue

Try reading this short exchange between a guest (**მუშგჳრი**, *mushgwri*), Giorgi, and his host, Bachana. Every line is a form recorded in the dictionary.

**Giorgi:** ხოჩა ლადეღ! (*Khocha ladegh!* — *Hello!*)
**Bachana:** ხოჩა ლადეღ! (*Khocha ladegh!* — *Hello!*)
*Bachana offers bread:* დჲა̈რ. (*Dyär.* — *Bread.*)
**Giorgi:** იჳასუ ხა̈რი! (*Iwasu khäri!* — *Thank you!*)
**Bachana:** ხოჩამდუ ხა̈რი! (*Khochamdu khäri!* — *Goodbye!*)
**Giorgi:** ხოჩამდუ ხა̈რი! (*Khochamdu khäri!* — *Goodbye!*)

### Next Steps
- Practice the word for "good": **ხოჩა** (*khocha*).
- Memorize the farewell **ხოჩამდუ ხა̈რი** (*khochamdu khäri*, "Goodbye").
- Stand in front of a mirror and say **ხოჩა ლადეღ** (*khocha ladegh*, "Hello"), then practise the wide "cat" vowel[^2] in **ნა̈ბოზ** (*näboz*, "evening").

Once you have mastered these mountain greetings, you have completed the current introductory modules for Svan!

### Grammar terms used in this lesson

[^1]: **Plural** (მრავლობითი რიცხვი, *mravlobiti ritskhvi*) — A grammatical form indicating more than one person or thing.
[^2]: **Vowel** (ხმოვანი, *khmovani*) — A speech sound made with your mouth open, not blocked by your teeth, tongue, or lips.
[^3]: **Root** (ძირი, *dziri*) — The most basic, irreducible part of a word that carries its fundamental meaning.
